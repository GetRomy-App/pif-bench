# Cedar Hollow Arts Center — Donor-Advised Fund Grants, Pledges and Event Payments

Gift Acceptance Policy, Part 4 · revised June 2025 · approved by the Finance Committee

## 4.1 Who the donor is

A grant from a donor-advised fund (DAF) is a gift from the **sponsoring organization** — for example, a community foundation or a national DAF sponsor — which has legal control of the fund's assets. The sponsoring organization is the legal donor of record.

- **Credit:** hard credit to the sponsoring organization; soft credit to the fund's advisor or advisors who recommended the grant.
- **Designation:** we honor the purpose stated in the sponsor's grant letter.

## 4.2 Acknowledging a DAF grant

1. The written acknowledgment goes to the **sponsoring organization**. It states the grant amount, the date received and that no goods or services were provided in exchange.
2. The advisor receives a **thank-you letter, not a receipt.** It may name the fund and the amount and thank the advisor for recommending the grant. It may not describe the grant as tax-deductible to the advisor, call itself a tax receipt, or include acknowledgment language addressed to the advisor as donor. The advisor received any income-tax deduction when contributing to the fund and receives no additional deduction for the grant.
3. If an advisor asks for a receipt for a DAF grant, we explain — warmly — that the sponsor is the legal donor and receives our acknowledgment.

## 4.3 No benefits from DAF grants

Federal law (Internal Revenue Code §4967) imposes penalty taxes when a DAF distribution provides an advisor, or a member of the advisor's family, with more than an incidental benefit. We treat gala seats, dinners and memberships as more than incidental. Our practical rule:

1. **DAF funds may not pay for any part of a ticket, table, sponsorship, membership or other benefit** — neither the full price nor the portion above fair market value. We do not split ("bifurcate") a benefit's price between a DAF grant and the advisor.
2. **An advisor who wants seats pays for them personally** (personal check, card or other personal funds — never a DAF). That payment is acknowledged to the advisor under §4.6.
3. We do not accept a DAF grant designated for an event purchase, and we never redirect any part of a DAF grant to one.

## 4.4 Pledges and statements of intent

1. Campaign commitments made on our current form (SW-SOI, rev. 2023-02, and later) are **non-binding statements of intent**. A DAF grant recommended by the person who signed the statement may be soft-credited toward it; the open balance is reduced by the full amount of the grant.
2. A commitment on the pre-2023 "Pledge Agreement" form is legally binding. Consult the CFO before soft-crediting any DAF grant toward it.
3. **We never reference an individual's pledge or statement of intent in correspondence with a DAF sponsor** — not in acknowledgments, grant requests, or inquiries about a grant's status.

## 4.5 Event purchases and campaign commitments

Payments for event tickets, tables and sponsorships are not applied to campaign pledges or statements of intent.

## 4.6 Quid pro quo acknowledgments

For any payment of more than $75 for which we provide goods or services, Internal Revenue Code §6115 requires a written statement that the amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of the payment over the value of the goods or services we provide, with our good-faith estimate of that value. (An organization that fails to make the disclosure is subject to a penalty of $10 per contribution, up to $5,000 per event or mailing.) Our acknowledgment states the payment, describes the goods or services, gives the CFO-approved fair market value, includes that disclosure and states the resulting deductible amount. Program listings are recognition and carry no value.
