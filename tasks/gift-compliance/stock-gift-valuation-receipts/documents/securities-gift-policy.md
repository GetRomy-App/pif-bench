# Bramblewood Land Trust — Gifts of Securities: Dating, Valuation and Receipts

Gift Acceptance Policy, Section 6 · revised September 2024 · approved by the Finance Committee · owner: Controller

## 6.1 What we accept

Publicly traded stock and mutual fund shares, delivered electronically to the Land Trust's brokerage account at Corliss & Pike Securities, transferred within a fund family, or sent as a stock certificate. We sell or redeem securities promptly after they reach our account.

## 6.2 Date of gift and tax year

We date every securities gift the way IRS Publication 526 dates it for the donor, so our records and the donor's return agree.

| How the shares come to us | Date of gift |
|---|---|
| Electronic transfer from the donor's broker (DTC), or a transfer between accounts at a mutual fund company | The date the shares are **credited to the Land Trust's account** — not the date the donor instructed the broker or fund, and not the date we receive a confirmation letter |
| Stock certificate, properly endorsed to the Land Trust (or with a signed stock power), **mailed** to us | The date of **mailing** — the postmark date |
| Certificate the donor gives to the donor's own broker or to the issuer's transfer agent to be reissued in our name | The date the shares are transferred to us on the corporation's books |

The **tax year** is the calendar year in which the date of gift falls. We do not date a gift earlier than this section allows, whatever the donor asks.

## 6.3 Value of the gift for our records

Following IRS Publication 561:

1. **Stock:** the fair market value is the **mean (average) of the highest and lowest quoted selling prices on the date of gift.** It is not the closing price.
2. **Mutual fund shares:** the fair market value is the **public redemption price** — the net asset value (NAV) per share — on the date of gift. It is not the public offering price, which includes a sales charge.
3. **Gift value** = shares (including fractional shares) × per-share value, rounded to the cent.
4. **What we do afterwards does not change the gift's value.** The price at which we sell, our net proceeds and any commission or fee are recorded by Finance as a separate sale; they are never used as the gift value.

## 6.4 Receipts for securities

Internal Revenue Code §170(f)(8) requires a contemporaneous written acknowledgment for a contribution of $250 or more. For property other than cash, the acknowledgment describes the property **but does not state its value**. Every securities receipt we send must:

1. name the donor;
2. describe the property: the **number of shares** (fractional shares in full) and the **name of the security** (issuer and class, or fund name and share class);
3. give the **date of gift** under §6.2;
4. state: "No goods or services were provided in exchange for your gift."

A securities receipt **never states a dollar value, a per-share price or an estimated value**, even when a donor asks. Valuing the gift for the donor's return is the donor's responsibility; staff do not give tax advice.

Model wording:

> Bramblewood Land Trust gratefully acknowledges your gift of [number] shares of [security], [credited to our account / mailed to us] on [date of gift]. No goods or services were provided in exchange for your gift.

## 6.5 Year-end

Gift entry for a calendar year closes on January 15 of the following year. Gifts are entered under their date of gift and tax year as determined in §6.2.
