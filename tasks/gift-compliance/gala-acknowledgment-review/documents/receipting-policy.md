# Fenwick Harbor Literacy Alliance — Gift Acknowledgment and Receipting Policy

Revised November 2025 · approved by the Finance Committee · owner: Chief Financial Officer

## 1. Scope

This policy governs every written acknowledgment the Alliance sends for a gift or for a payment connected with a fundraising event. Staff do not depart from it without the CFO's written approval.

## 2. When we acknowledge

2.1 Every contribution receives a written acknowledgment, whatever its amount, within 30 days of receipt.

2.2 Payments connected with a benefit event (gala tickets, sponsorships, paddle-raise gifts, raffle purchases, gifts made at the event) are acknowledged in one batch after the event, once the CFO has approved the final good-faith estimates of the benefits provided. The CFO or the Director of Development reviews every event batch before it is sent.

## 3. The federal rules we follow

3.1 **Written acknowledgment — Internal Revenue Code §170(f)(8).** A donor may not deduct a single contribution of $250 or more unless the donor has a contemporaneous written acknowledgment from us. The acknowledgment must state:

- (a) the amount of cash contributed, or a description (but not the value) of any property other than cash;
- (b) whether the Alliance provided any goods or services in consideration, in whole or in part, for the contribution; and
- (c) if we did, a description and a good-faith estimate of the value of those goods or services (or, if they consist solely of intangible religious benefits, a statement saying so).

We apply these content rules to every acknowledgment, including those for gifts under $250.

3.2 **Quid pro quo disclosure — Internal Revenue Code §6115.** When we receive a payment of more than $75 that is partly a contribution and partly a payment for goods or services we provide, we must give the donor a written statement that:

- (a) informs the donor that the amount of the contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of the money contributed over the value of the goods or services we provided; and
- (b) gives our good-faith estimate of the value of those goods or services.

The $75 test is applied to the payment the donor makes, not to the deductible portion of it. An organization that fails to make a required disclosure is subject to a penalty of $10 for each contribution, up to $5,000 for any one fundraising event or mailing.

3.3 **Raffles.** Amounts paid for raffle tickets are not deductible charitable contributions: the buyer pays for a chance to win a prize. A raffle purchase is never included in a contribution amount, and no acknowledgment states a deductible amount for it.

## 4. Valuing what a donor receives

4.1 Our good-faith estimate is the fair market value of the goods or services — what they would cost a member of the public in a comparable commercial setting — not their cost to us and not the price we charge for a ticket. For each event the CFO approves a per-guest value for each benefit. Acknowledgments use those values.

4.2 Listing a donor's or sponsor's name in a printed program, on signage, on a slide or in an annual report is donor recognition. It is assigned no value and is not described as a benefit. (The Alliance does not sell program advertising.)

4.3 A sponsorship package is valued benefit by benefit: every seat in the package carries the per-guest seat value, and every other benefit (for example, a VIP reception for a number of guests) carries its own approved per-guest value.

4.4 A seat that a sponsor fills with a guest is the sponsor's benefit and is valued on the sponsor's acknowledgment. It is not deducted from any separate gift the guest makes.

## 5. Declined and unused benefits

Following Revenue Ruling 67-246:

5.1 A donor who **declines the benefit in writing at the time of payment** — for a gala, by checking the reply card's "I will not attend; please do not reserve a seat" box — and for whom no seat is reserved has received no goods or services. The full payment is a contribution, and the acknowledgment states that no goods or services were provided.

5.2 A donor who pays for a ticket and does not attend, or who asks after the event to have the payment treated as a gift, **still received the benefit**: the seat was reserved and available to them. The deductible amount is not increased, and we do not issue or revise an acknowledgment to show a larger deductible amount because a benefit went unused.

## 6. Combined transactions

When one payment combines a gift with a purchase (raffle tickets, tickets, an auction item), the acknowledgment treats each part separately. The contribution amount excludes any raffle purchase. Where a raffle purchase appears on an acknowledgment at all, the acknowledgment says that raffle ticket purchases are not charitable contributions and are not deductible.

## 7. Acknowledgment checklist

**Every acknowledgment must state:**

1. the donor's name and the date the payment was received;
2. the amount of cash received (for property, a description only — see §3.1(a));
3. either "No goods or services were provided in exchange for your contribution", **or** a description of the goods or services provided, our good-faith estimate of their value, the §3.2 disclosure sentence where the payment is more than $75, and the resulting deductible amount ("Your deductible contribution is $…").

**No acknowledgment may:**

1. describe a payment as fully deductible, or show a deductible amount equal to the payment, when goods or services were provided;
2. state a deductible amount for raffle tickets, or count a raffle purchase in a contribution amount;
3. state a value for property other than cash;
4. offer tax advice (for example, whether a donor can itemize or what a donor's accountant should claim).
