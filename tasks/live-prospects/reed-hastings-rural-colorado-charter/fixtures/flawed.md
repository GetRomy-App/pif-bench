# Donor Intelligence Report: Reed Hastings

Prepared April 25, 2026 for a charter school network expanding into rural Colorado. Bracketed numbers refer to the sources at the end.

## Summary

Reed Hastings co-founded Netflix and is now its executive chairman. In April 2026 he said he will leave the Netflix board in June to focus on philanthropy [2][3]. He and his wife, Patty Quillin, are among the largest funders of charter schools in the country. They give mostly through intermediaries: their donor-advised Hastings Fund at the Silicon Valley Community Foundation (SVCF), The City Fund, and the Charter School Growth Fund (CSGF). Hastings sits on CSGF's board, and CSGF is headquartered in Denver [5][6][9][10]. His most direct Colorado tie is Lone Rock, an educators' retreat in rural Bailey, Park County, that he financed [12][13]. He has the capacity for a gift of $10–25 million to an organization like ours. The fastest path is a direct written proposal to him.

## 1. Personal and professional background

- **Personal.** Hastings is 65 [1]. He is married to Patty Quillin, his partner in giving; they have two children and live in Santa Cruz, California [4][8].
- **Education.** B.A. in mathematics from Bowdoin College (1983); Peace Corps high-school math teacher in Swaziland; M.S. in computer science (artificial intelligence) from Stanford [1][11].
- **Career.**
  - Founded Pure Software in 1991 and took it public in 1995 [1].
  - Co-founded Netflix in 1997 and was its CEO from the late 1990s until January 2023. From 2020 he was co-CEO with Ted Sarandos [1].
  - Since January 2023 he has been Netflix's Executive Chairman, the role he holds today [1][2].
- **Leaving Netflix.** On April 16, 2026 Netflix disclosed that Hastings will not stand for re-election at its June 2026 annual meeting. He remains executive chairman until then; the shareholder letter says he is leaving "to focus on his philanthropy and other pursuits" [2][3].
- **Other roles.**
  - Current: CEO of Powder Mountain, a Utah ski resort; director of Bloomberg L.P. [1].
  - Past: California State Board of Education (2000–2004), Microsoft board (2007–2012), Facebook board (2011–2019) [1].
- **Education boards.** CSGF board member as of April 2026 [9]. His CSGF and KIPP bios (undated) also list KIPP Foundation, Pahara Institute, National Alliance for Public Charter Schools and The City Fund.

## 2. Wealth indicators and asset profile

- **Netflix equity is the core of his wealth.** As of April 6, 2026 he beneficially owned 37,759,062 Netflix shares, including options on 16,595,546 shares; the Hastings-Quillin Family Trust holds 21,159,576 shares. That is under 1% of the company [1]. These counts reflect Netflix's 10-for-1 split of November 2025 [16].
- **Value of the stake.** At the April 24, 2026 close of $92.44, the trust shares are worth about $1.96 billion [1][17], and the options add to that.
- **Liquidity.** He exercises options and sells the shares every month. On April 1, 2026, for example, that came to roughly $40 million gross [7].
- **Net worth.** Forbes put him at about $4.1 billion after his January 2024 stock gift; its 2026 real-time estimate is about $4–5.5 billion [8][18]. Bloomberg estimated $6.6 billion just before that gift [19].
- **Other assets.** He became majority owner of Powder Mountain (Eden, Utah) in September 2023, after a reported $100 million investment [1][20].

## 4. Cause areas and giving philosophy

- **K-12 education reform** is the center of his giving: charter schools, and the "portfolio" model of school autonomy with accountability [10][14]. He also funds leadership development for educators (Lone Rock, Pahara) [12].
- **Other causes.** College access for Black students (UNCF, Spelman, Howard) [8], and AI and the humanities at his alma mater [11].
- **How he gives.** Big money moves through vehicles and intermediaries: his donor-advised fund, The City Fund and CSGF. Few of his documented gifts go straight to an individual school network [6][9][10]. The Giving Pledge letter frames his giving as helping "our community, our country and our planet" [4].

## 5. Estimated giving capacity: single gift to an education nonprofit

**$10–25 million.** That is a realistic ceiling for a K-12 organization, though he has made documented single gifts of $40 million to each of three education institutions (2020) and $50 million to Bowdoin (2025) [8][11]. In 2024 he moved about $1.6 billion of Netflix stock to charity, including about $1.1 billion into his donor-advised fund [6][7]. He still holds a trust stake worth about $2 billion plus options [1].

## 6. Recommended ask and engagement strategy

**Recommended ask:** a $1–2.5 million multi-year lead gift for the rural Colorado expansion, to be made after cultivation. It should be tied to a defined proof point, such as opening two rural campuses with published outcome targets. His documented K-12 giving runs mostly through national intermediaries, so a network new to him should expect a first gift far below his capacity.

**Engagement strategy:**

1. **Write to him directly.** Send Hastings a two-page proposal and cover letter from the CEO now, asking for a meeting. CSGF is headquartered in Denver, and he is a board member and a $10M-plus funder [9]; mention CSGF in the letter.
2. **Use the Pahara and Lone Rock network.** Pahara fellowships and Lone Rock convenings build familiarity with his circle [12]. The Lone Rock Foundation makes grants only to preselected organizations, so do not send it a proposal [13][21].
3. **Follow up.** If there is no reply within a month, follow up by email.
4. **Timing.** He leaves the Netflix board in June 2026 to focus on philanthropy [3]. Plan a first conversation for the second half of 2026.
5. **Framing.** The City Fund's model is built for cities [10]. Present the rural case with outcome data, autonomy-with-accountability, and good relations with local districts.

## 7. Key connection points and potential red flags

**Connection points**

- CSGF in Denver: board seat and $10M+ funder [9].
- Lone Rock in Bailey, Park County: a Colorado mountain community where he hosts educator retreats [12].
- Lone Rock Foundation officers [13]:
  - Hastings is president.
  - Kim Smith, Pahara's founder, is treasurer.
  - A Kevin Hall is secretary; CSGF's CEO is also named Kevin Hall [9], so confirm whether they are the same person.
- KIPP Foundation and Pahara Institute ties [8][12].

**Red flags**

- **School governance.** In 2014 he called elected school boards a fundamental problem for school districts, which drew a public rebuttal from the California School Boards Association [22]. Rural Colorado communities tend to value local boards, so expect scrutiny of any link to him.
- **Lone Rock's history.** It was built in secrecy under confidentiality agreements and met local opposition during Park County permitting [12]. Be careful how the network references it locally.
- **The City Fund's politics.** The City Fund and its political arm spend on school-board races, which critics contest [15].
- **Giving style.** He rarely gives to single networks directly, so expect intermediary diligence and slower timelines.
- **Netflix.** He is leaving the board, so do not use Netflix as a channel.
