## 3. Roles

| Fact | As of | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Museum of Modern Art:** International Council from 1967; trustee from 1976; vice president 1988–1991; **president 1991–2002** (an unpaid post held 11 years); also elected chairman on November 10, 1993, holding the dual posts of president and chairman 1993–1995. Afterwards President Emerita (from 2005 per The Art Newspaper — year not graded) and Life Trustee until her death. Oversaw the $858 million Taniguchi expansion (completed 2004) and the 1999 merger with P.S. 1 (now MoMA PS1). She was never MoMA's founder or director. | 2025-09-19 | high | MoMA press release 1993-11-10 (above); NYT obituary; LDF statement; The Art Newspaper obituary |
| MoMA's director since September 2025 is Christophe Cherix; Glenn D. Lowry stepped down that month. | 2025-09-22 | high | NYT obituary correction of 2025-09-22 (Bowdoin reprint) |
| **Founded Studio in a School in 1977**, with her own money, after New York City's fiscal-crisis budget cuts virtually eliminated art from the public schools; it places professional artists in public-school classrooms. It began in three schools (two in the Bronx, one in Manhattan) and now serves schools in all five boroughs; an affiliate, Studio Institute, works nationally. Studio in a School lists her as its founder; the Center for Curatorial Leadership lists her as "Founder and Chair Emerita, Studio in a School". | 2026-04-19 | high | https://www.studioinaschool.org/about/; https://www.studioinaschool.org/remembering-our-founder/ (archived 2026-04-19: https://web.archive.org/web/20260419085043/https://studioinaschool.org/remembering-our-founder/); NYT obituary; https://www.curatorialleadership.org/about-us/board-trustees/ |
