# Harborview Arts Center — Advancement Policy DR-7: When a Donor Dies

Approved by the Vice President for Advancement and the Chief Financial Officer · Revised 2025-07-01

This policy applies to every member of the Advancement and Finance staff. Where it names a person by title, that person (or someone they designate in writing) acts, and no one else.

## 1. Confirming a death

A death is confirmed by any one of: (a) an obituary or death notice in a newspaper or on a funeral home's website that matches at least two identifiers in our record (full name, home address or town, spouse's name, date of birth or age); (b) a probate court filing for the donor's estate; or (c) word from a family member or the funeral home. A staff member who learns of a donor's death tells the Advancement Services Manager and the donor's relationship manager the same day.

## 2. Updating our records

Within two business days of confirmation, the **Advancement Services Manager**:

1. marks the record deceased and enters the date of death;
2. closes every open proposal with the status "Closed — donor deceased" and cancels open actions and meetings with the donor;
3. removes the decedent from every appeal, mailing, e-mail list and event invitation, including any mail file not yet sent to the printer;
4. changes the household's addressee and salutation to the surviving spouse alone; and
5. flags the records of the surviving spouse, the children, and any foundation or business in which any of them serves as a trustee, director or officer: "Bereavement — no solicitation before [date]" (see Section 3).

## 3. The bereavement period

No one at the Center may solicit the surviving spouse, any child of the donor, or any foundation or business flagged under Section 2.5 before the **180th day after the date of death**. Solicitation may resume on that day, and not before.

"Solicitation" includes: personal asks; proposals; grant requests and letters of request or inquiry to a foundation; appeal letters and e-mails; phone calls asking for a gift; event sponsorship and paddle-raise asks; and pledge reminders.

Condolence and stewardship contact is encouraged during the bereavement period, but it may not include any discussion of gifts, proposals, pledges or the estate. If a family member raises a gift on their own initiative, staff may listen and answer questions, must not present a proposal or suggest an amount, and must report the conversation to the Vice President for Advancement the same day.

A proposal closed under Section 2.2 is not transferred to a family member. A new proposal to a surviving family member may be opened only after the bereavement period, with the approval of the Vice President for Advancement.

## 4. Condolences

- The **relationship manager** calls the family or sends a personal handwritten note within two business days of confirmation, and may attend the visitation or funeral.
- The Center's condolence letter is prepared by the **Donor Relations Manager** and signed by the **President & CEO**. When the decedent was a current or former member of the Center's Board of Trustees, the letter is co-signed by the **Chair of the Board of Trustees**.

## 5. Open pledges

The unpaid balance of a decedent's pledge is the amount pledged less the payments Finance has received and applied to that pledge. Gifts that were not applied to the pledge do not reduce the balance.

Within five business days of confirmation, the relationship manager refers the unpaid balance to the **CFO**, attaching the pledge agreement and payment history and stating: the unpaid balance; the probate case number; the name of the personal representative (executor); and the deadline for filing claims against the estate.

Only the CFO, or counsel the CFO engages, decides whether to present a claim to the estate and communicates with the personal representative or the estate's attorney about the pledge. Development staff never ask the surviving spouse, the children, a family foundation or the personal representative to pay a decedent's pledge, and no pledge reminder is sent to the household.

## 6. Bequest intentions and other planned gifts

The relationship manager notifies the **Director of Planned Giving**, who alone contacts the personal representative or the estate's attorney to learn whether the will or trust includes a gift to the Center. A bequest intention is revocable. It is recorded as an expectancy and is not booked as revenue, as a receivable or as campaign credit until the estate's distribution is received.

## 7. Memorial gifts

The **Donor Relations Manager** sets up a memorial fund code for the fund named by the family, acknowledges every memorial gift to its donor, and sends the next of kin a list of memorial donors' names and addresses once a month. The amounts of memorial gifts are never shared with the family.

## 8. Naming in memory

The naming of a space in memory of a decedent requires a gift agreement signed after the bereavement period. Staff do not propose a memorial naming to the family during the bereavement period.
