# Salem Creek Youth Theatre — Prospect Research Ethics and Privacy Standard

Adopted by the Board Development Committee · Revised 2025-11-03 · Modelled on the APRA Statement of Ethics

This standard governs everyone who researches prospects or writes donor profiles for Salem Creek Youth Theatre (SCYT). A donor profile that does not meet it does not go to a gift officer.

## 1. Principles

- **Integrity.** We obtain information lawfully and openly, and we never misrepresent who we are or why we are asking.
- **Relevance.** We record only what bears on a prospect's connection to SCYT, their philanthropic interests and their capacity to give.
- **Accuracy.** Every fact carries its source. We keep facts and estimates apart, and we do not record what we cannot source.
- **Privacy.** Information being public does not make it ours to record. A prospect's family, health and private life are not research subjects.
- **Confidentiality.** Profiles go only to staff who need them for the prospect's relationship with SCYT.

## 2. Sources

**Permitted sources:** county property and tax records; deed and mortgage records; NC Secretary of State business filings; SEC filings; FEC records; IRS Forms 990 and 990-PF; published donor lists, annual reports and honor rolls; news media; professional licensing boards; a person's own public biography (a company website or a public LinkedIn profile); and SCYT's own records.

**Disallowed sources:** people-search and background-check sites and other data brokers (any site that sells or aggregates contact details, relatives, "possible" addresses or "estimated" wealth or income); private or friends-only social-media content, however it was seen; information obtained by pretext; and leaked or hacked material.

A fact found only in a disallowed source is not recorded in a profile — not as a fact, a lead or an "unverified" note — and is not used in any calculation. If a researcher later confirms it in a permitted source, the profile may record it and cite that source.

## 3. What never goes in a profile

The following never appear in a donor profile, even when the information is public and even in indirect or softened wording:

- (a) health information about the prospect or any family member, including diagnoses, surgery, treatment, recovery, disability or medical leave, and any crowdfunding or "family support" campaign raised for a medical need;
- (b) mental-health information, wherever it was found, including in court records;
- (c) family-law matters — divorce, separation, custody, child support, adoption or protective orders — and any family-court or domestic case;
- (d) the name, age, grade, school or activities of a minor child (under 18), except as Section 4 allows;
- (e) religious affiliation, sexual orientation and immigration status;
- (f) political opinions, party affiliation or ideology, and any messaging or framing advice based on them.

Leave this material out entirely. Do not summarize it, allude to it or list it in the profile, not even as "excluded" or "not used." The researcher records exclusions in the research log, which gift officers do not see.

## 4. Allowed only in a set form

- **Minor children:** the profile may state how many minor children the prospect has and, if a minor child takes part in an SCYT program, the name of the program (for example, "a minor child is enrolled in SCYT's Summer Intensive"). Nothing else.
- **Adult children:** name, city and occupation may be recorded.
- **Political giving:** one line only. It gives the total of the prospect's federal contributions itemized by the FEC in the **four most recent full calendar years**, with the years and "Source: FEC" — for example, "Federal political contributions, 2022–2025: $X (Source: FEC)." It names no recipients, candidates, committees, parties or causes and carries no commentary. State and local political contributions are not recorded.
- **Private businesses:** ownership, title, founding year, and published revenue or headcount, each with its source. Never estimate the value of a privately held company or of the prospect's stake in it, and never estimate a prospect's net worth or income.

## 5. Capacity method

**Five-year giving capacity = 5% of real estate + 5% of public securities + 2 × documented giving**

- **Real estate:** the current county tax value of each property owned by the prospect alone, jointly with a spouse, or by an LLC that a permitted record shows the prospect owns as sole member or majority owner. Property owned by an LLC with other members is excluded unless a permitted record shows the prospect holds a majority. Values come only from county tax records.
- **Public securities:** holdings in SEC filings that name the prospect. Equity in a privately held company is not counted.
- **Documented giving:** the total of the prospect's documented gifts (alone or with a spouse) to charitable organizations, SCYT included, dated in the **five most recent full calendar years**. Gifts and sponsorships made by the prospect's company are business giving and are not counted, even when SCYT's database soft-credits them to the prospect. Where a donor list gives only a range, use the bottom of the range.

## 6. Capacity rating

| Rating | Five-year capacity |
|---|---|
| A | $500,000 and above |
| B | $250,000 – $499,999 |
| C | $100,000 – $249,999 |
| D | $25,000 – $99,999 |
| E | under $25,000 |

## 7. Profile format

1. Summary
2. Identity and household
3. Career and business
4. Real estate
5. Philanthropy and affiliations
6. Political giving (the single line Section 4 allows)
7. Capacity calculation (line by line) and rating
8. Sources

Cite the source of every fact.
