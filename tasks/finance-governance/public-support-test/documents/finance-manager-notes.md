# Notes on the revenue and donor files — Schedule A support data, 2021–2025

From: Nadia Brecht, Finance Manager, Alder Fork Watershed Council
Date: 2026-09-10

The two CSVs are pulled from our general ledger and donor database for calendar years 2021–2025, on the accrual method we use for the Form 990. Notes on the lines that need explaining:

1. **Contributions and grants.** `revenue-by-source-2021-2025.csv` shows every contribution and grant by ledger line. `donors-10k-plus-2021-2025.csv` breaks the same money out by donor for everyone who gave $10,000 or more in total over the five years; the last row is everyone else. The two files reconcile year by year. No donor on the list is related to any other donor on the list — Dellwood Paper Company is a publicly held company and is not controlled by any of our individual donors, and the Kowalczyks give jointly and are already listed as one donor.

2. **Marguerite Ostrander** is our founding donor. Her gifts are annual gifts for general operations, by check.

3. **Benedict Sarkis** gave $35,000 in 2023 toward the Cedar Flats culvert replacement; his other gifts are annual-fund gifts.

4. **Hollins-Pryor Family Foundation** and **Juniper Ridge Fund** are private non-operating foundations (both file Form 990-PF). Hollins-Pryor's 2023 and 2024 grants funded the riparian planting program.

5. **Upper Alder Community Foundation** is a community foundation recognized under section 170(b)(1)(A)(vi) (IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search, checked 2026-09-09). All three grants came from its competitive Watershed Fund, decided by its own grants committee. None came from a donor-advised fund, and no donor earmarked any of them for us.

6. **Oregon Department of Environmental Quality** grants are restoration grants under the state's nonpoint-source program. We design and run the projects; DEQ receives no services from us. They are grants, not contracts.

7. **Kettle County water-quality monitoring contract.** The county's Public Works Department pays us a fixed fee per sampling round for the quarterly stream sampling it needs for its stormwater permit; the data belong to the county. Our auditors classify it as a fee-for-service contract (program service revenue), alongside our workshop and guided-trip fees.

8. **Realized gain on sale of securities, 2024.** In March 2024 we sold publicly traded stock that Marguerite Ostrander gave us in 2019 and that we had held in our investment account. Proceeds were $278,500; our basis (value when given) was $96,500; the gain was $182,000. The 2019 gift itself is outside the five-year period.

9. **Interest and dividends** are from our operating reserve, held in a brokerage account. We have no rents, royalties or unrelated business income.

10. **Unusual grants.** Ferrante & Lowe reviewed our largest gifts with us in August. None qualifies as an unusual grant — Ostrander's gifts recur every year — so no contribution is excluded on that basis.

11. **Volunteers and in-kind.** About 2,300 volunteer hours a year and some donated native plants are not recorded as revenue and are not in either file. No governmental unit furnishes us services or facilities free of charge, and no tax is levied for our benefit.
