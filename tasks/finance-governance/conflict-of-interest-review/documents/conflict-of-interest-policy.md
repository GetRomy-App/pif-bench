# Crescent Harbor Children's Theatre — Conflict of Interest Policy

Adopted by the Board of Directors 2019-09-17. Last revised 2024-06-11.

## Article I — Purpose

This policy protects the Theatre's interests when it considers a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of a director, officer or committee member, and supports the Theatre's compliance with the laws governing charitable organizations. It supplements, and does not replace, applicable state and federal law.

## Article II — Definitions

**1. Interested Person.** Any director, principal officer, or member of a committee with board-delegated powers who has a direct or indirect Financial Interest.

**2. Financial Interest.** A person has a Financial Interest if the person has, directly or indirectly, through business, investment, or a Family Member:

- (a) an ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the Theatre has a transaction or arrangement;
- (b) a compensation arrangement with the Theatre, or with any entity or individual with which the Theatre has a transaction or arrangement; or
- (c) a potential ownership or investment interest in, or compensation arrangement with, any entity or individual with which the Theatre is negotiating a transaction or arrangement, including any entity that has submitted a bid or proposal to the Theatre.

A Financial Interest is not necessarily a conflict of interest. Under Article III, Section 2, a person with a Financial Interest has a conflict of interest only if the board decides that a conflict exists.

**3. Family Member.** A spouse or domestic partner; ancestors; brothers and sisters (whole or half blood) and their spouses; and children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren and their spouses.

## Article III — Procedures

**1. Duty to disclose.** An Interested Person must disclose the existence of the Financial Interest and all material facts — in writing to the board chair before the meeting at which the transaction is considered, and orally at that meeting — and must be given the opportunity to disclose them to the directors considering the transaction.

**2. Determining whether a conflict exists.** After the disclosure and any discussion with the Interested Person, the Interested Person leaves the meeting while the remaining directors discuss and vote on whether a conflict of interest exists.

**3. Addressing the conflict.**

- (a) The Interested Person may make a presentation, but must then leave the meeting during the discussion of, and the vote on, the transaction. The Interested Person does not vote on the transaction.
- (b) The chair shall, if appropriate, appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives.
- (c) After exercising due diligence, the board determines whether the Theatre can obtain, with reasonable efforts, a more advantageous transaction or arrangement from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict of interest.
- (d) If a more advantageous transaction is not reasonably possible, the board determines, by a majority vote of the disinterested directors present, whether the transaction is in the Theatre's best interest, for its own benefit, and fair and reasonable, and decides accordingly whether to enter into it.

**4. Competitive bids.** A contract of $10,000 or more with an entity in which an Interested Person has a Financial Interest requires at least two competing written bids, which the disinterested directors compare before they vote.

## Article IV — Violations of the policy

**1. Notice and opportunity to explain.** If the Governance Committee has reasonable cause to believe a director has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall inform the director in writing of the basis for that belief and give the director an opportunity to explain the failure to disclose, in writing or at a meeting of the committee, within 15 days of the notice.

**2. Finding and corrective action.** After hearing the director's response and making any further investigation the circumstances warrant, the Governance Committee reports its findings to the board. If the board, acting by a majority of the disinterested directors, determines that the director failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action, which may include a written reprimand, removal from committee or officer positions, a request for resignation, or removal under Bylaws Article IV, Section 7.

**3. Re-review of the transaction.** Any transaction approved without the disclosure and recusal this policy requires shall be re-reviewed by the disinterested directors under Article III, Sections 3(c) and 3(d). If the re-review finds that the Theatre paid more than fair market value, the board shall consult legal counsel on whether the payment may be an excess benefit transaction under Internal Revenue Code section 4958 and on recovering the excess.

**4. Recusal of the director under review.** The director whose conduct is under review takes no part in the Governance Committee's or the board's deliberations or votes under this Article, including as a member of the Governance Committee.

**5. Corrected statement.** The director shall file a corrected annual statement (Article VI) within 30 days of the board's determination.

## Article V — Records of proceedings

The minutes of the board and of every committee with board-delegated powers shall record: the names of persons who disclosed or were found to have a Financial Interest, and the nature of the interest; any action taken to determine whether a conflict was present, and the board's decision; and the names of the persons present for the discussion and votes on the transaction, the content of the discussion (including alternatives considered), and a record of the vote.

## Article VI — Annual statements

Each director, principal officer and committee member signs a statement every January affirming that he or she has received, read and understood this policy and agrees to comply with it, and disclosing any Financial Interest — his or her own or a Family Member's — in any entity that does business with the Theatre or has submitted a bid or proposal to it. A director whose circumstances change during the year updates the statement promptly.

## Article VII — Form 990 reporting

The Controller gives the Theatre's Form 990 preparer, each year, a list of every transaction during the fiscal year with a director, officer or key employee, with their Family Members, and with entities they or their Family Members own or control, so that Schedule L and Part VI of the Form 990 are completed accurately.

## Bylaws excerpt (Article IV, Section 5 — Voting)

A motion of the board carries by a majority of the votes cast at a meeting at which a quorum (seven directors) is present. A tie vote fails.
