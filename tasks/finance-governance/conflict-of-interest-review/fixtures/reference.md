## 1. Catering — Saffron & Sage Events LLC (Thomas Aldana)

**The interest.** Saffron & Sage Events LLC's sole member and manager (100%) is Celeste M. Aldana, at 88 Orchard Terrace, Crescent Harbor [documents/state-business-registry.md]. That is Thomas Aldana's home address on the board roster [documents/board-roster-2025-26.md], and his annual statement names his spouse as Celeste Aldana, "self-employed" [documents/annual-statements-2026.md]. She signed the company's bid, its contract and its W-9 [documents/gala-bids-and-contracts.md]. A spouse is a Family Member under the policy, so Mr. Aldana had a Financial Interest in the catering contract (Article II, Sections 2–3) — an actual or possible conflict of interest that he was required to disclose so the board could decide whether a conflict existed.

**What the policy required.**
- Disclose the interest and all material facts in writing to the board chair before the meeting, and orally at it (Article III, Section 1); and report it on his January annual statement (Article VI).
- Leave the meeting while the other directors determined whether a conflict existed and while they discussed and voted on the contract, and not vote (Article III, Sections 2–3).
- Because the contract was over $10,000, the disinterested directors had to compare competing bids and decide whether a more advantageous arrangement was available (Article III, Sections 3(c) and 4).

**What happened.**
- **Annual statement:** on 2026-01-20 he answered "No" to Question 2 — whether he or a Family Member has an interest in any business that has submitted a bid to the Theatre — although Saffron & Sage had submitted its bid on 2026-01-08 [documents/annual-statements-2026.md; documents/gala-bids-and-contracts.md].
- **Meeting:** on 2026-02-12, after the Chair asked for disclosures, the minutes record that no director other than Ms. Ramaswamy disclosed anything. Mr. Aldana then spoke for Saffron & Sage ("a cut above"), moved to award it the contract, and voted for his own motion [documents/board-minutes-2026-02-12.md].
- **Price:** the board rejected the Gala Committee's recommendation of Harborview Provisions, the lowest bid at $52,900, and awarded the contract at $68,400 — **$15,500 (29.3%) more** — and $12,650 more than Tidewater Catering's $55,750, all for the same menu and bar specification. With 12 additional guests and an on-site bar upgrade, the Theatre paid Saffron & Sage **$71,860** in fiscal 2026 [documents/payment-register-fy2026-gala.md].
- **Decisive vote:** the motion carried **6–5** with Mr. Aldana voting in favor. Without his vote it would have been 5–5, and under the bylaws a tie fails (Bylaws Article IV, Section 5). The contract was approved only because of the vote of the director with the undisclosed interest.

## 4. What the policy requires next (Article IV)

1. **Notice and opportunity to explain (Section 1).** The committee informs Mr. Aldana in writing of the basis for its belief that he failed to disclose a possible conflict — the registry record, his January answer, and the February 12 minutes — and gives him the opportunity to explain, in writing or at a committee meeting, within 15 days of the notice. No finding about his conduct, and no view about whether the omission was deliberate, should be reached before he has responded.
2. **Findings to the board (Section 2).** After hearing his response and any further investigation, the committee reports its findings to the board.
3. **Board determination and corrective action (Section 2).** If the board, acting by a majority of the disinterested directors, determines that he failed to disclose, it takes appropriate disciplinary and corrective action — options in the policy include a written reprimand, removal from committee positions, a request for resignation, or removal under Bylaws Article IV, Section 7.
4. **Re-review of the catering contract (Section 3).** The disinterested directors re-review the contract under Article III, Sections 3(c) and 3(d): whether a more advantageous arrangement was available — the record shows written bids of $52,900 and $55,750 for the same specification — and whether the contract was in the Theatre's best interest, fair and reasonable. If they find the Theatre paid more than fair market value, the board must consult legal counsel on whether the payment may be an excess benefit transaction under Internal Revenue Code section 4958 and on recovering the excess. That is a question for counsel; this memo does not conclude that an excess benefit transaction occurred.
5. **Corrected statement (Section 5).** Mr. Aldana files a corrected annual statement within 30 days of the board's determination.
6. **Records (Article V).** The committee's and the board's minutes record each step.
