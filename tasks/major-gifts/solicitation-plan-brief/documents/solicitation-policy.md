# Wren Harbor Library Foundation — Major Gift Solicitation Policy

rev. 2025-12 · adopted by the Board of Directors · applies to every ask of $25,000 or more

## 1. Sizing the ask

1.1 Start from the **five-year capacity estimate** in the prospect's current research profile.

1.2 Multiply it by the **inclination factor** for the prospect's current inclination score:

| Inclination score | Factor |
|---|---|
| 5 — committed | 0.30 |
| 4 — strong | 0.25 |
| 3 — moderate | 0.15 |
| 2 — weak | 0.10 |
| 1 — none | Do not solicit |

1.3 Round the result to the nearest $5,000.

1.4 **Cap.** The ask may not exceed four times the household's largest single gift to the Foundation in the five years before the date of the plan. If the rounded figure is above the cap, the ask is the cap, rounded down to the nearest $5,000.

1.5 **Household gifts** are gifts from either spouse's personal funds, gifts the couple recommended from a donor-advised fund, and gifts paid by their family foundation. These are **not** household gifts: gifts or sponsorships paid by a business the prospect owns or works for (those are corporate gifts, even when soft-credited to the prospect), event ticket purchases, and gifts to other organizations.

1.6 The plan states the ask as one figure, not a range, and shows how it was sized.

## 2. Purpose of the gift

The plan proposes a purpose the prospect has shown interest in, as recorded in contact reports. It never proposes a purpose the prospect has said they will not support, whatever the Foundation's current campaign priorities.

## 3. The solicitation team

3.1 For an ask of $50,000 or more, the relationship manager is joined by a **leadership partner**: the Foundation's Executive Director, or a board member who (a) has made their own gift to the Foundation in the current fiscal year and (b) is free of any conflict under the Conflict of Interest Policy.

3.2 If the prospect has asked for a particular person, and that person meets 3.1, that person is the leadership partner.

## 4. Couples

The ask is addressed to the partner who makes the household's philanthropic decisions, as recorded in contact reports — not necessarily the person listed first in the CRM.

## 5. Donor preferences

The most recent preferences recorded in contact reports — how and where to reach the prospect, when they make giving decisions, how they wish to be recognized — govern. They override older CRM fields until Advancement Services updates the record.

## 6. The solicitation plan brief

The relationship manager and the leadership partner work from one brief, which states:

1. the ask amount and how it was sized (section 1);
2. the purpose proposed (section 2);
3. who will be asked, and who makes the ask (sections 3–4);
4. timing and setting;
5. how the meeting will be arranged;
6. recognition;
7. talking points;
8. follow-up.

The brief is shared with the leadership partner, who may be a volunteer, so it is bound by the Donor Confidentiality and Recognition Policy.
