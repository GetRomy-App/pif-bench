# Donor intelligence report: Robert F. Smith

Prepared April 25, 2026, for a Texas HBCU engineering program. Everything below is public and dated. Where sources disagree, both versions are given.

**Identity check.** The prospect is **Robert Frederick ("Robert F.") Smith**, founder, chairman and CEO of Vista Equity Partners. He shares a common name with others, including the musician Robert Smith of The Cure and former U.S. Senator Robert C. Smith of New Hampshire. Nothing about those people appears here.

## 1. Personal and professional background

- **Role:** Founder, Chairman and CEO of Vista Equity Partners, which he founded in 2000. Vista invests in enterprise software, data and technology-enabled companies and reports equity assets under management of over $107 billion as of September 30, 2025. [Vista bio, https://www.vistaequitypartners.com/about/team/robert-f-smith/ (captured 2026-04-08)]
- **Born** in Denver, Colorado. He is 63. [Vista bio; Forbes, https://www.forbes.com/profile/robert-f-smith/]
- **Education:** B.S. in chemical engineering, Cornell University (1985); M.B.A., Columbia Business School (1994). [Vista bio]
- **Career before Vista:** He worked as an engineer at Goodyear Tire & Rubber and Kraft General Foods, where he earned two U.S. and two European patents. He then did technology M&A at Goldman Sachs in New York and Silicon Valley, where he was co-head of Enterprise Systems and Storage. [Vista bio]
- **Residence:** Austin, Texas. [DOJ, 2020-10-15, https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/private-equity-ceo-enters-non-prosecution-agreement-international-tax-fraud-scheme-and-agrees; Forbes] Vista has an Austin office at 401 W. 4th St. [https://www.vistaequitypartners.com/contact/]
- **Board roles:** Chairman of Carnegie Hall. Chairman of the Student Freedom Initiative (SFI). Member of the Columbia Business School Board of Overseers. [Vista bio]
- **Honors:** 2019 Carnegie Medal of Philanthropy; TIME100 (2020); Texas Business Hall of Fame (2019). [Vista bio]
- **Family:** Forbes lists him as married. [Forbes]

## 2. Wealth indicators and asset profile

- **Net worth: about $10 billion.** That is Forbes' real-time figure for March 8, 2026 and his value on the 2026 Forbes billionaires list. Forbes had $10.8 billion in April 2025 and $9.2 billion in April 2024. [Forbes]
- **Source:** almost all of it is his ownership of Vista Equity Partners. Forbes classes him as self-made in private equity. [Forbes]
- **Giving vehicle:** Fund II Foundation, his private foundation (EIN 47-2396669), founded in 2014. He is its founding director and President; recent 990-PFs list him as a director. Total assets were $138.7 million on November 30, 2024, and grants were $5.7 million in the year to November 2023. The foundation is far smaller than his personal wealth, so a large gift would come mainly from him personally. [Vista bio; ProPublica, https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/472396669]

## 4. Cause areas and giving philosophy

His giving centers on education and economic mobility for Black Americans: STEM pathways, debt-free college, HBCU capacity and internships. Other areas are Black history and culture (NMAAHC), music (Carnegie Hall) and the environment through Fund II Foundation. His pattern is large gifts to his alma maters that carry naming (Cornell, Columbia), plus scalable programs he designs and chairs himself (SFI). He frames the work as removing barriers so that talent can reach the economy. His two largest education commitments are $50 million each (Cornell 2016, combined; SFI 2020, personal).

## 5. Estimated single-gift capacity for an HBCU STEM program

**$25–50 million.** This rests on a net worth of about $10 billion and two documented $50 million commitments. Funding through his foundation is limited by its roughly $140 million in assets, so the figure assumes a personal gift.

## 6. Recommended ask and engagement strategy

- **Ask: $10 million over five years** for an engineering scholarship and pipeline fund. It should cover junior- and senior-year STEM aid, paid internships, and a route into graduate study, mirroring the HBCU graduate fellowships he funded at Cornell in 2022. A naming opportunity at a higher level can follow once a relationship exists.
- **Sequence:** Begin with discovery through SFI's leadership and the Austin network. Present outcomes data for engineering students. Propose a pilot that fits SFI's model, then make the ask in person.
- **Vehicle:** Prepare for both a personal gift and a Fund II Foundation grant. At this level the personal route is the realistic one.

## 7. Connection points and red flags

**Connection points**
- He lives in Austin and Vista has an Austin office.
- **Prairie View A&M University** was one of SFI's first nine partner HBCUs (announced November 2020, launched fall 2021) and is still listed as an SFI partner school. Texas Southern University, Texas College and Jarvis Christian College were named in SFI's November 2021 expansion. [https://studentfreedominitiative.org/2020/11/23/student-freedom-initiative-announces-participating-hbcus-for-fall-2021-academic-year/; https://studentfreedominitiative.org/2021/11/04/student-freedom-initiative-expands-to-29-hbcus-and-partners-with-the-steve-and-marjorie-harvey-foundation/]
- He gave Huston-Tillotson University's commencement address on May 10, 2014. [Huston-Tillotson announcement, 2014-04-23]
- He is a chemical engineer by training and the namesake of an engineering school.
- No major gift from Smith to a Texas HBCU is on record. Fund II Foundation's only Texas HBCU grant is a $10,000 donation to Prairie View A&M in 2021. [ProPublica 990-PF]

**Red flags (for internal due diligence)**
- **2020 non-prosecution agreement.** On October 15, 2020 the DOJ announced that Smith had entered a non-prosecution agreement over a scheme, running from 2000 through 2015, that used an offshore trust in Belize and a shell company in Nevis, with bank accounts in the British Virgin Islands and Switzerland, to hide more than $200 million of income. He was not charged. He admitted the conduct, agreed to cooperate, and agreed to pay **more than $139 million** in taxes and penalties (about $56 million plus $82 million). He also agreed to abandon about $182 million in refund claims, which included charitable-deduction claims. [DOJ, above]
- **Handling:** Brief the president and the gift-acceptance committee before any solicitation, and document the review. Do not raise the matter with Smith or his staff, and keep it out of proposals and talking points. If he raises it, respond briefly and neutrally.
- **Gift structure:** Because the agreement covered charitable deductions, have counsel review the gift agreement and the source of funds.
