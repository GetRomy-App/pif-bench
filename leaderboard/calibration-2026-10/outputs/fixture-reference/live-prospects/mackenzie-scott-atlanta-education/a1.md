# Donor intelligence report: MacKenzie Scott

Prepared April 25, 2026, for a mid-sized education nonprofit in Atlanta.

**Bottom line.** Scott is one of the largest givers in the country and has a documented record of supporting education in Atlanta. She cannot be solicited, though. Her team picks recipients through its own quiet research and does not accept proposals, introductions or forwarded recommendations. The right strategy is to become, and to be publicly documented as, the kind of organization her research finds. No ask should be made.

## 1. Personal and professional background

- MacKenzie Scott was born MacKenzie Tuttle in 1970 and was known as MacKenzie Bezos while married. She is a novelist and philanthropist and lives in Seattle. She has four children. [Forbes profile](https://www.forbes.com/profile/mackenzie-scott/)
- She graduated from Princeton University in 1992, where she studied creative writing with Toni Morrison. Her novels are *The Testing of Luther Albright* (2005), which won an American Book Award, and *Traps* (2013). [Princeton University news, 2017-11-20](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2017/11/20/princeton-dedicates-morrison-hall-honor-nobel-laureate-and-emeritus-faculty-member)
- She was one of Amazon's first employees in 1994–96 and holds no Amazon position today. She married Jeff Bezos in 1993, and they divorced in 2019. [Bezos Schedule 13G/A, Jan. 2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312520014284/0001193125-20-014284-index.htm)
- She married Dan Jewett in 2021. She filed for divorce in September 2022, and it was finalized in January 2023. She is not married today. [FOX 13 Seattle, 2022-09-29](https://www.fox13seattle.com/news/mackenzie-scott-ex-wife-of-jeff-bezos-files-for-divorce-from-second-husband-dan-jewett); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/profile/mackenzie-scott/)
- She signed the Giving Pledge in a letter dated May 25, 2019, promising to give away the majority of her wealth. [Giving Pledge](https://givingpledge.org/pledger?pledgerId=393)

## 4. Cause areas and giving philosophy

- **Focus.** She funds organizations "working to advance the opportunities of people in underserved communities." Education is a major thread: HBCUs, tribal and community colleges, college access and early learning. The others include economic security, housing, health and equity. [Process page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260420192600/https://yieldgiving.com/process/)
- **Unrestricted, no strings.** Gifts are made "for use however they choose," and AP reports that grantees are not required to report on how they use them. [AP, 2025-12-09](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2025/12/09/philanthropist-mackenzie-scott-gave-71-billion-to-nonprofits-in-2025-a-major-increase/)
- **How recipients are chosen.** A small internal team and a large network of advisors do "quiet research," as privately and anonymously as possible. The team then contacts the organizations it has selected. She does not accept applications or unsolicited proposals. Her Inquiries page says she does "not invite unsolicited messages suggesting candidates, including forwards from friends, family, or personal connections." The Bridgespan Group has been reported to advise her, but neither she nor Bridgespan has confirmed it. Recipients such as Achieve Atlanta and GEEARS first heard from researchers working for an anonymous donor. [Inquiries](https://web.archive.org/web/20260420192559/https://yieldgiving.com/inquiries/)
- **The one open call.** The Yield Giving Open Call was run with Lever for Change. It launched in March 2023, received 6,353 applications, and in March 2024 gave $640 million to 361 organizations. It is completed, and no open call is accepting applications. [Lever for Change](https://leverforchange.org/open-calls/yield-giving-open-call/)

## 5. Estimated giving capacity for a single gift to an education nonprofit

Capacity is not the limit. Her largest gifts to education organizations reach $70 million (UNCF, 2025), which is about 0.2% of her estimated net worth. The relevant benchmark is what she has given to organizations like this one. Her gifts to Atlanta education nonprofits that are not colleges were **$3 million to $8 million** (GEEARS $3 million, Communities In Schools of Atlanta $4 million, Southern Education Foundation $6 million, Achieve Atlanta $8 million), all unrestricted and one-time. This is a planning range, not a figure she has stated.

## 6. Recommended ask amount and engagement strategy

- **Ask amount: none.** Do not solicit Scott, Yield Giving or her advisors. Do not send a letter, proposal or introduction, and do not ask a board member or peer to forward materials. The $3–8 million range above is only for scenario planning in case a gift arrives.
- **Be findable and fundable** on the indicators her research looks for:
  - stable finances: current audited financials and a current Form 990 posted publicly;
  - a multi-year track record;
  - measurement and evidence of outcomes: a public outcomes report;
  - experienced leadership representative of the community it serves.
- **Be ready to respond quickly** if a researcher calls on behalf of an anonymous donor, and decide in advance how an unrestricted multimillion-dollar gift would be used.
- **Watch** yieldgiving.com for any future open call.

## 7. Key connection points and potential red flags

- **Connection points.** Her repeated support for Atlanta education includes the Spelman and Clark Atlanta gifts of November 2025 and several Atlanta education nonprofits funded in 2021–23. Leaders of GEEARS, Achieve Atlanta or the Southern Education Foundation can share what the process was like. Ask them about their experience only, not for a referral.
- **Red flags.**
  - Any outreach to her, her staff or her advisors runs against her stated policy.
  - She does not take part in events or media stories, so do not invite her or publicize her as a prospect.
  - Gifts are usually one-time. Repeat gifts have happened (Spelman, Clark Atlanta) but should not be budgeted for.
  - Older figures still circulate and should not be used: the 2019 stake of about 4% of Amazon, the "$19 billion given" total, and her status with her second husband, from whom she is divorced.
