**Re:** Whether the Council passes the one-third public support test on the 2025 Form 990

## The answer

**Yes — we pass the 33⅓% test for 2025, but narrowly.** Our public support percentage for 2021–2025 is **34.33%**, about **one percentage point** above the 33⅓% line. In dollars, public support of $1,260,596 is **$36,729** more than one-third of total support ($1,223,867). We qualify as a publicly supported organization on line 16a without needing the facts-and-circumstances test.

The reason it is close: under the 2% rule, each donor counts as public support only up to $73,432 over the five years, while every dollar a donor gives counts in full toward total support. Marguerite Ostrander's $2,025,000 is 55% of our five-year total support, so as her giving grows our percentage falls.

## What is left out, and why

Total ledger revenue for 2021–2025 is $4,308,000. Two items in it are not support for this test:

- **Program service revenue, $454,400** — landowner workshop fees ($118,500), guided river trip fees ($79,900) and the Kettle County water-quality monitoring contract ($256,000). These are gross receipts from related activities; they go on line 12 for information and are not part of public support or total support. The county contract pays us for sampling the county needs for its own permit, so it is a fee for services, not a government grant.
- **Realized gain on the March 2024 sale of securities, $182,000** — a gain on the sale of a capital asset, which is excluded from total support. (The underlying stock was Ms. Ostrander's 2019 gift, which is outside the five-year period.)

Reconciliation: $4,308,000 − $454,400 − $182,000 = **$3,671,600** total support.

No contribution has been treated as an unusual grant: Ferrante & Lowe reviewed the largest gifts in August and found that none qualifies, because Ms. Ostrander's gifts recur every year [documents/finance-manager-notes.md].

## For the board

- We pass for 2025, by about one percentage point.
- The margin is thin and depends on how much of our support comes from donors below the $73,432 limit, from the community foundation and from DEQ. Broadening the base of gifts from many donors is what moves the percentage up; larger gifts from donors already above the limit move it down.
