# Donor Intelligence Report: Laurene Powell Jobs

Prepared April 25, 2026, for a mid-sized Washington, D.C. journalism and media-literacy nonprofit. Bracketed numbers refer to the sources listed at the end.

## Summary

- **Who she is.** Founder and president of Emerson Collective, an LLC that combines grant-making, venture investing and advocacy [1][2]. She is the widow of Apple co-founder Steve Jobs [3].
- **Wealth.** $21.4 billion (Bloomberg Billionaires Index, April 2026) [3]. The fortune was inherited, mostly as Apple and Disney shares [4][3].
- **Journalism giving.** She has a documented record of funding nonprofit newsrooms, including ProPublica, The Texas Tribune, Chalkbeat, The 19th and The Current. Emerson reports roughly $37 million to nonprofit journalism from 2015 to early 2021 [5][6].
- **D.C. ties.** She controls The Atlantic, which is headquartered in Washington [5][7][8]. College Track, which she co-founded, runs a Southeast D.C. center [9].
- **How to approach her.** A letter of inquiry to Emerson's media and journalism team, backed by a warm introduction where possible.
- **Recommended ask:** $300,000 over three years ($100,000 a year) in general operating support.

## 1. Personal and professional background

- Born in New Jersey in 1963. She is 62 [3][4].
- University of Pennsylvania: a BA, and a BS in economics from Wharton (1985). MBA, Stanford Graduate School of Business (1991) [4][11].
- She worked at Merrill Lynch Asset Management and then at Goldman Sachs as a fixed-income trading strategist. She co-founded Terravera, a natural-foods company [3][11].
- She married Steve Jobs in 1991. He died on October 5, 2011, and they had three children. She lives in Palo Alto [3][4][11].
- **Current roles:** founder and president of Emerson Collective; majority investor and board chair of The Atlantic; co-founder and board member of College Track and of the Waverley Street Foundation; co-founder and board chair of the XQ Institute [1][2][12].

**Source of wealth.** The fortune was inherited from Steve Jobs, principally Walt Disney and Apple shares [3][4]. Bloomberg reports that her family trusts held about 4% of Disney as of a January 2017 SEC filing [3].

**Holdings**

- **The Atlantic.** Emerson Collective agreed to buy a majority stake in July 2017 [7]. The stake is reported at about 70%, for about $110 million [5]. This is a for-profit investment, not a gift.
- **Monumental Sports & Entertainment** (Wizards, Capitals, Mystics). She holds a stake of about 20%, bought in 2017 [4]. At the group's reported $7.2 billion valuation it is worth roughly $1.4 billion [14]. It is her largest D.C. asset.
- **Other media investments** (all for-profit):
  - Axios. Emerson exited when Axios was sold to Cox Enterprises in 2022 [15].
  - The Athletic and Ozy Media [5].
  - Pop-Up Magazine. Emerson cut ties in 2020 [5].
  - Anonymous Content and Concordia Studio [2][5].

## 3. Philanthropic giving history

**Vehicles**

- **Emerson Collective: LLC, not a foundation.** It has no Form 990 and no public grant list. It does not disclose individual grant amounts, so documented gifts understate her giving [2][5].
- **Waverley Street Foundation: her climate foundation.** It is a 501(c)(3) private foundation (EIN 81-3242506), organized in 2016 as the Emerson Collective Foundation [6][16].
  - In 2021 she committed $3.5 billion over ten years. It is a spend-down foundation [6][17].
  - FY2024 Form 990-PF: assets of $2.74 billion and charitable disbursements of $306.2 million. She is vice chair [16].
  - It funds climate work only: clean energy and healthy land and food [17].
- **Donor-advised fund.** Bloomberg reports more than $2 billion given through Emerson and a Goldman Sachs donor-advised fund [3]. A gift may arrive through a DAF.
- **XQ Institute.** She co-founded it in 2015 with Russlynn Ali, and she chairs it. It redesigns high schools [12].
- **College Track.** She co-founded it in 1997. It is a ten-year college-completion program for under-resourced students [11][22].

## 4. Cause areas and giving philosophy

- **Causes:** education, immigration, climate, media and journalism, and health [4][2].
- **Philosophy.** She uses every tool available: grants, investment and advocacy [2].
  - Grants are often multi-year, unrestricted operating support with light reporting [20].
  - Her giving is deliberately private [5].
- **Journalism.** Emerson funds investigative, local and public-interest newsrooms. It says it has "zero say" in the editorial decisions of outlets it backs [5].
- **Media literacy.** I found no documented grant from her to a media-literacy organization. The fit runs through her journalism and education work (XQ, College Track).

## 5. Estimated giving capacity

- **Capacity is not the limiting factor.**
  - She is worth about $21.4 billion [3].
  - Waverley Street alone disbursed $306 million in 2024 [16].
  - She made a $4.6 million grant to a national newsroom [5].
- **Estimated single-gift capacity to a journalism nonprofit: $1 million or more.**
- **What limits the gift is her pattern.** Documented grants to mid-sized and local newsrooms run from $100,000 to $350,000 a year [5][18].

## 6. Recommended ask and engagement strategy

**Ask: $300,000 over three years ($100,000 a year) in general operating support.**

- The anchor is her grants to comparable newsrooms:
  - The 19th: $100,000 [5].
  - The Current: $170,000 over two years [18].
  - The Texas Tribune and Chalkbeat: $200,000–$350,000 a year [5].
- Move toward $350,000 a year only if Emerson signals interest in a larger D.C. program.

**Strategy**

1. **Send a two-page letter of inquiry** to Emerson Collective's media and journalism team (info@emersoncollective.com) [10]. Follow it with a full proposal for the $300,000 request.
2. **Build a warm introduction.**
   - Ask leaders of her grantee newsrooms, such as the American Journalism Project network, for an introduction.
   - Evan Smith of Emerson Collective is active in that network [21].
   - Map board and donor ties to Emerson's media staff.
3. **Lead with D.C. accountability reporting and young readers.** A media-literacy partnership with College Track Southeast D.C. students is a natural opening [9].
4. **Keep The Atlantic's newsroom out of the approach.** Emerson stresses editorial independence [5].
5. **Prepare for her terms:** multi-year unrestricted support, a possible DAF payment, and no public recognition unless she agrees [3][5].

## 7. Key connection points and potential red flags

**Connection points**

- The Atlantic, which she controls, is headquartered in Washington, D.C. [7][8].
- College Track Southeast D.C. (Ward 8) opened in January 2020 [9].
- Her stake of about 20% in Monumental Sports & Entertainment, owner of the Wizards, Capitals and Mystics, puts her among D.C.'s civic and business leaders [4].
- Her grantee newsrooms and the American Journalism Project network [5][19].

**Red flags**

- **Opacity.** As an LLC, Emerson does not publish its grants, and gifts may come through a DAF. The documented record is a floor [3][5].
- **Perceived conflict.** She owns a major D.C. news outlet. Make editorial independence explicit in any agreement [5].
- **Unproven media-literacy interest.** Frame the program as journalism and civic education.
