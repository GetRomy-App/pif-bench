# Donor intelligence report: Agnes Gund

Prepared April 25, 2026 for a nonprofit arts education program serving New York City public school students.

## Bottom line

**Agnes Gund died on September 18, 2025, at her home in Manhattan, aged 87** ([NYT obituary](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/19/arts/agnes-gund-dead.html), reprinted in full by [Bowdoin](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/); [NAACP LDF statement](https://www.naacpldf.org/news/ldf-mourns-the-loss-of-agnes-gund-devoted-philanthropist-and-advocate-for-social-good/)). She can't be cultivated or asked for a gift. Her philanthropy now runs through the **AG Foundation**, whose surviving trustees are her daughters **Catherine Gund and Anna Traggio**, and through her estate, which is still being settled. The foundation's largest grant in 2023 went to **Studio in a School**, the NYC public-school arts-education nonprofit Gund founded. That shows the family's deep commitment to NYC public-school arts education, a natural fit for our program.

## 1. Personal and professional background

- Born August 13, 1938, in Cleveland. Her father, George Gund II, built a fortune in real estate, brewing and investing and was for many years president of the Cleveland Trust Company ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/); [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/09/19/agnes-gund-philanthropist-collector-obituary)).
- She earned a B.A. in history from Connecticut College for Women (1960) and an M.A. in art history from Harvard (Fogg), which the NYT dates to 1980 ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/); [MoMA, 1993](https://www.moma.org/docs/press_archives/7201/releases/MOMA_1993_0108_66.pdf)).
- **Museum of Modern Art:** she joined the International Council in 1967 and became a trustee in 1976. She was **president from 1991 to 2002** and also chairman from 1993 to 1995. Afterwards she was President Emerita and Life Trustee until her death ([MoMA, 1993](https://www.moma.org/docs/press_archives/7201/releases/MOMA_1993_0108_66.pdf); [NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/); [LDF](https://www.naacpldf.org/news/ldf-mourns-the-loss-of-agnes-gund-devoted-philanthropist-and-advocate-for-social-good/)).
- **Studio in a School:** she **founded it in 1979**, with her own money, after New York City's fiscal-crisis budget cuts virtually eliminated art from the public schools. It places professional artists in public-school classrooms. It started in three schools and now works across all five boroughs ([Studio in a School](https://www.studioinaschool.org/about/); [NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/)).
- She co-founded the Center for Curatorial Leadership in 2007 and received the National Medal of Arts in 1997 ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/); [NEA](https://www.arts.gov/honors/medals/agnes-gund)).
- **Family:** four children from her marriage to Albrecht Saalfield: Catherine Gund (a filmmaker), David, Jessica Saalfield and Anna Traggio. She also had twelve grandchildren ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/)).

## 2. Wealth indicators and asset profile

- **Source:** inherited wealth. Her father died in 1966 and left her a large fortune in trust ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/)).
- **Art collection:** she paid for much of her philanthropy by selling art. In 2017 she privately sold Roy Lichtenstein's *Masterpiece* (1962) for $165 million ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/)).
- **Estate:** in February 2026 her estate consigned three works to Christie's for its May 2026 New York sales. They are a Rothko (estimate about $80 million), a Twombly ($40–60 million) and a Cornell ($3–5 million), at least about $123 million together. The proceeds are to settle her estate. Most of her remaining collection had already been given or promised to museums ([Boston Globe/NYT, Feb. 14, 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/02/14/nation/christies-to-auction-3-of-agnes-gunds-art-jewels); [Galerie](https://galeriemagazine.com/agnes-gund-art-collection-going-up-for-auction-at-christies/)).
- **AG Foundation (EIN 34-1606084):** it holds about $95 million in assets ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/341606084); [2024 Form 990-PF](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/341606084/202503179349100835/full)).

## 4. Cause areas and giving philosophy

Her causes were contemporary art and museums, arts education for NYC public-school children, criminal-justice and racial-justice reform, and later reproductive rights. Her method was to sell art she loved to fund causes she cared about ([NYT](https://obituaries.bowdoin.edu/agnes-gund-h12/); [Art for Justice](https://artforjusticefund.org/)).

## 5. Estimated giving capacity

**Personal capacity: not applicable.** She has died. The relevant capacity is the AG Foundation's. It holds about $95 million in assets and paid $3.9 million in grants in 2024 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/341606084)). Its only seven-figure grants in 2023 went to Studio in a School ($1,085,000) and Rockefeller Philanthropy Advisors ($1,000,000) ([2023 Form 990-PF](https://apps.irs.gov/pub/epostcard/cor/341606084_202312_990PF_2025011022984261.pdf)). Its 2023 grants to other NYC arts-education groups ran from $3,000 to $15,788 (table above).

## 6. Recommended ask and engagement strategy

1. **No ask to Agnes Gund.** Remove her from solicitation lists, and plan no cultivation, ask or bequest request addressed to her.
2. **Letter of inquiry.** Submit a letter of inquiry to the AG Foundation through its open application process, addressed to its surviving trustees, **Catherine Gund and Anna Traggio** ([2024 Form 990-PF](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/341606084/202503179349100835/full)). Ask board members, artists or educators who know them to mention it.
3. **Lead with alignment.** Present the program as continuing the work Gund began at Studio in a School, whose model the family knows well ([Studio's 2024 Form 990](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/133003112)).
4. **Ask, if invited:** $10,000–$25,000 for a defined program. That is at or above the foundation's 2023 grants to comparable NYC arts-education groups, with room to grow.
5. **Timing:** her estate is being settled through the Christie's sale. Don't press the family during that period.

## 7. Connection points and red flags

**Connection points**
- **Catherine Gund** chairs the George Gund Foundation and is a trustee of the AG Foundation ([George Gund Foundation](https://gundfoundation.org/our-team/); [2024 Form 990-PF](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/346519769)). **Anna Traggio** is a trustee of both.
- Shared networks: Studio in a School's board, MoMA, and the Center for Curatorial Leadership.
- **Affinity:** her foundation gave Studio in a School $3.28 million over 2020–2023, which shows how much the family values NYC public-school arts education.

**Red flags**
- **She has died** (September 18, 2025). Any outreach addressed to her would be a serious error.
- **Shrinking grant budget.** AG Foundation grants fell from $35.3 million in 2020 to $8.1 million in 2023 and $3.9 million in 2024 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/341606084)).
- **The George Gund Foundation is not a route for this program.** Its arts grants go to organisations in or directly serving the City of Cleveland. It does not prioritise arts education outside the Cleveland Metropolitan School District ([Culture + Arts guidelines](https://gundfoundation.org/culture-arts/)).
- **The Art for Justice Fund is closed** (June 30, 2023).
- **Don't credit her with her brothers' gifts.** The Gund at Kenyon College honours her brother Graham ([Kenyon](https://www.kenyon.edu/news/archive/kenyon-remembers-graham-gund-63-h-81/)). The Foundation Fighting Blindness was co-founded by her brother Gordon; she was a supporter ([FFB](https://www.fightingblindness.org/news/agnes-gund-renowned-philanthropist-social-justice-champion-and-art-patron-passes-away-2851)).
