## 2. Garrison Ridge Foundation (Rank 1)

Suggested by Mr. Ibarra, who understood that it funds basic-needs programs in south-central Pennsylvania [documents/board-minutes-excerpt.md].

**Grants paid, tax year 2025** [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md]

- **Total paid:** $480,000. This matches total disbursements of $648,300 less operating and administrative expenses of $168,300.
- **Number of grants paid:** 13.
- **Grants to Pennsylvania organizations:** 12. The one non-Pennsylvania grant was Mason-Dixon Food Share in Westminster, MD.
- The $60,000 to Lebanon Valley Food Hub and the $75,000 to Harrisburg Youth Mentoring Collaborative are approved for future payment. They are not counted above.

**Fit**

- **Mission: fit.**
  - "Basic needs — food access and nutrition" is a stated priority [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md].
  - Food-access grants paid include a $35,000 mobile market for fresh produce at Southern Lancaster County Food Network (Quarryville, PA). They also include a $15,000 SNAP outreach and enrollment grant at Upper Dauphin Food Pantry [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md].
- **Geography: fit.**
  - The website names Lancaster County among its giving counties [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md].
  - The 990-PF grants-paid list includes Pennsylvania recipients [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md].
- **Type of support: program only.**
  - Every grant paid in 2025 was for programs or operations. None was for land, buildings, construction, renovation, equipment or vehicles [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md].
  - The website says grants "support programs and the organizations that run them." It does not expressly invite capital requests [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md].
  - Under Section 5 of the policy, we ask Garrison Ridge only for the **program component**: the southern-county mobile markets [documents/grants-policy.md].

**How and when it accepts requests**

- **Process.** Garrison Ridge uses two steps:
  - First, a letter of inquiry of no more than two pages, submitted through the online portal.
  - Then, an invited full proposal about seven weeks later. Full proposals are not accepted without an invitation [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md].
- **Where the 990-PF and the website differ.**
  - The 990-PF says LOIs are accepted on a rolling basis [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md].
  - The website says that since January 2026, LOIs are reviewed in two cycles: spring (LOI due March 1) and fall (LOI due September 1) [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md].
  - Section 1 of the policy says to follow the website [documents/grants-policy.md].
- **Fall 2026 cycle.** The fall LOI deadline closed on September 1, 2026 [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md]. The files do not show whether Millstream submitted a fall LOI.
- **Next deadline after this assessment: March 1, 2027, 5:00 p.m. Eastern** (spring cycle LOI).
  - March 1, 2027 is a Monday, so the weekend rule does not apply.
  - Invited full proposals would be due in mid-April 2027, with decisions in June 2027 [documents/garrison-ridge-website.md].
  - Mobile markets are scheduled to start in June 2027 [documents/project-summary.md]. A June 2027 decision would therefore arrive at about the same time as the program launch.

**Board or staff relationship**

- No Millstream board member or staff member appears among Garrison Ridge's directors, officers or executive director [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md; documents/board-roster.md].

**Ask range (program component)**

- **Comparable grants.** These are grants paid in 2025 to Pennsylvania organizations working in food access, hunger relief or nutrition [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md]. Sorted by amount:

| Recipient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Upper Dauphin Food Pantry | $15,000 |
| Hanover Area Food Share | $20,000 |
| Carlisle Community Meals | $25,000 |
| Gettysburg Area Nutrition Program | $30,000 |
| Southern Lancaster County Food Network | $35,000 |
| York Fresh Start Pantry | $40,000 |
| Lebanon Valley Food Hub (year 1 payment only) | $60,000 |

- **Excluded:**
  - Mason-Dixon Food Share, because it is in Maryland.
  - Grants for non-food purposes: mentoring, clinic, literacy, reentry and diapers.
  - Lebanon Valley Food Hub's year-2 future payment.
- **Bottom of range:** the smallest comparable grant, $15,000.
- **Top of range:** the median of the seven grants (the fourth when sorted), $30,000. This is well below the $260,000 remaining program need [documents/project-summary.md].
- **Ask range: $15,000–$30,000 for the southern-county mobile markets.**

**Recommended approach**

1. Prepare a two-page LOI for the mobile-market program and submit it through the portal by **March 1, 2027**.
2. Garrison Ridge already funds a fresh-produce mobile market run by Southern Lancaster County Food Network in Quarryville [documents/garrison-ridge-990pf-2025.md]. Fresh Routes' markets will serve six southern-county townships [documents/project-summary.md]. The LOI should explain how the two efforts relate. The files do not say whether the service areas overlap.
3. Do not ask Garrison Ridge for capital support.

## 3. Marwood Family Foundation (Rank 2)

Suggested by Ms. Pfautz, who noted that it has paid for freezers, a building and a refrigerated van at county pantries [documents/board-minutes-excerpt.md].

**Grants paid, tax year 2025** [documents/marwood-990pf-2025.md]

- **Total paid:** $708,000. This matches total disbursements of $804,400 less operating and administrative expenses of $96,400.
- **Number of grants paid:** 10.
- **Grants to Pennsylvania organizations:** all 10.

**Fit**

- **Mission: fit.**
  - The website lists "feeding neighbors who are hungry" in Lancaster County as a priority.
  - It also says many grants help organizations "build, repair and equip the places where they serve" [documents/marwood-website.md].
- **Geography: fit.**
  - The website names Lancaster County [documents/marwood-website.md].
  - Every 2025 grant went to a Pennsylvania organization [documents/marwood-990pf-2025.md].
- **Type of support: fit for both components.**
  - Capital grants paid include a walk-in freezer and cold-storage expansion, a building purchase, a refrigerated van, and a loading dock and shelving.
  - Program and operating grants were also paid [documents/marwood-990pf-2025.md].

**How and when it accepts requests**

- **It does not accept requests.**
  - The 990-PF box is **checked**: the foundation "only makes contributions to preselected charitable organizations and does not accept unsolicited requests for funds."
  - The application contact, form, deadlines and restrictions are not completed [documents/marwood-990pf-2025.md].
  - The website says the foundation "does not accept unsolicited proposals, letters of inquiry or applications," and that grant requests sent to its address "will not be considered" [documents/marwood-website.md].
- **Next deadline:** none.

**Board or staff relationship**

- **Margaret W. Lindqvist** of Lititz, PA, a Millstream Director, is listed as a **Trustee** of the Marwood Family Foundation, averaging 1.0 hour per week [documents/marwood-990pf-2025.md; documents/board-roster.md].
- Ms. Lindqvist was absent from the September 10 meeting [documents/board-minutes-excerpt.md].

**What the policy requires**

- Section 2 applies. Millstream sends no unsolicited proposal, LOI, application or other written request for funds, from staff or from any board member on Millstream's behalf. The strong mission fit Ms. Pfautz described does not change this [documents/grants-policy.md].
- Under Section 3.2, Ms. Lindqvist's relationship is the **only** approach:
  - She may ask Marwood's trustees whether the foundation would welcome a conversation with Millstream or would invite a request.
  - Staff prepare and send nothing to the foundation unless it invites a request **in writing**.
- Under Section 3.3, Ms. Lindqvist takes no part in Marwood's decision on any Millstream request [documents/grants-policy.md].

**Ask range**

No request may be made now, so no ask is recommended. If Marwood invites a request in writing, the Section 6 range would be as follows.

- **Comparable grants.** These are grants paid in 2025 to Pennsylvania food-access or hunger-relief organizations [documents/marwood-990pf-2025.md]. Sorted by amount:

| Recipient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Manheim Community Food Cupboard | $25,000 |
| Mount Joy Area Pantry | $45,000 |
| Elizabethtown Neighbors Food Shelf | $48,000 |
| Lititz Meals on Wheels | $60,000 |
| Ephrata Area Community Pantry | $85,000 |
| Columbia Hope Kitchen | $150,000 |

- **Excluded:** tenant assistance, reading program, free clinic and youth center.
- **Bottom of range:** $25,000.
- **Top of range:** the median of six grants, the average of $48,000 and $60,000, which is $54,000. This is within both the $490,000 capital need and the $260,000 program need [documents/project-summary.md].
- **Reference range, if invited: $25,000–$54,000.** Either component is eligible. The cold-storage grants above suggest the capital component.

**Recommended approach**

1. The board chair asks Ms. Lindqvist whether she is willing to raise Millstream with her fellow trustees, and briefs her on the September 10 discussion.
2. No other board member and no staff member contacts the foundation about funding.
3. Staff act only on a written invitation from Marwood.

## 4. Calloway-Deane Charitable Trust (Rank 3)

Suggested by Dr. Calloway, who had read that it makes grants for hunger relief and fresh-food access, including refrigerated trucks [documents/board-minutes-excerpt.md].

**Grants paid, tax year 2025** [documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md]

- **Total paid:** $390,000. This matches total disbursements of $488,450 less operating and administrative expenses of $98,450.
- **Number of grants paid:** 12.
- **Grants to Pennsylvania organizations: 0.**
  - Every recipient is in North Carolina, South Carolina or Virginia.
  - Two recipient names could be mistaken for Pennsylvania organizations. **Lancaster County Hunger Coalition** is in **Lancaster, SC**. **Western York County Food Pantry** is in **York, SC**.

**Fit**

- **Mission: strong fit.**
  - The trust funds food banks, pantries, mobile markets and food rescue, including "programs, operations, equipment and facilities" [documents/calloway-deane-website.md].
- **Geography: not a fit.**
  - The grants-paid list contains no Pennsylvania recipient [documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md].
  - The published guidelines do not name Lancaster County or Pennsylvania [documents/calloway-deane-website.md; documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md].
  - The corporate trustee's Philadelphia mailing address does not establish fit under Section 4 [documents/grants-policy.md].
  - The guidelines state no geographic limit. The 990-PF restrictions mention only 501(c)(3) status and no grants to individuals.
  - The trust's history describes a grocery chain that served the Carolinas and Virginia [documents/calloway-deane-website.md].
- **Type of support: fit for both components, if geography is resolved.**
  - Capital grants paid include a refrigerated warehouse expansion ($90,000) and a refrigerated truck ($65,000).
  - Program and operating grants were also paid [documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md].

**How and when it accepts requests**

- The 990-PF box is **not** checked [documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md].
- Applications are submitted online. The application opens March 1 and September 1, with deadlines of **April 15** (decisions in June) and **October 15** (decisions in December).
- The website says grants typically range from $10,000 to $90,000 [documents/calloway-deane-website.md].
- The 990-PF and the website agree on the deadlines [documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md; documents/calloway-deane-website.md].
- **Next deadline after this assessment: October 15, 2026.** The following one is April 15, 2027.

**Board or staff relationship**

- None is shown in the files.
- The trust's trustees are Keystone Fiduciary Trust Company, Harriet Deane Pruitt and Samuel R. Deane [documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md]. None is a Millstream board member or staff member [documents/board-roster.md].
- The files do not show any connection between Dr. Owen M. Calloway and the trust's founder, Thomas A. Calloway [documents/calloway-deane-website.md]. A shared surname alone is not a Section 3 relationship.
- Keystone Fiduciary Trust Company is a different company from Keystone Cold Logistics, Mr. Fernández's employer [documents/board-roster.md].

**Ask range**

- **None.** The trust made no comparable grants, meaning no grants to Pennsylvania food-access organizations.
- Section 6 therefore requires that no ask range be set and that the question be referred to the Executive Director, Thomas Greider [documents/grants-policy.md; documents/board-roster.md].
- The website's typical range of $10,000–$90,000 is not a basis for an ask range under the policy.

**Recommended approach**

1. No proposal or application is prepared now.
2. Because the guidelines state no geographic limit, the Director of Development may make **one** inquiry by email (grants@callowaydeanetrust.org) or telephone ((215) 555-0143). The inquiry asks whether the trust would consider a request from a Pennsylvania organization. The answer is recorded [documents/grants-policy.md; documents/calloway-deane-website.md; documents/calloway-deane-990pf-2025.md].
3. Only a **written** yes makes the trust a geographic fit. If one arrives:
   - The trust becomes eligible for a request.
   - Sizing goes to the Executive Director, because there are no comparable grants.
   - The October 15, 2026 deadline is usable only if the written yes and the Executive Director's decision both arrive in time. Otherwise the target is April 15, 2027.
4. If the answer is no, or no written answer comes, the trust remains not a fit and no further contact is made.
