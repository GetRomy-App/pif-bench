## 2. What the policy requires

### Definitions

**Financial Interest** [documents/conflict-of-interest-policy.md, Art. II §2]. A director has a Financial Interest if, directly or through a Family Member, the director has either of the following:

- an ownership interest in an entity with which the Theatre has a transaction; or
- a potential ownership interest in an entity that has submitted a bid to the Theatre.

**Family Member** [documents/conflict-of-interest-policy.md, Art. II §3]. This term includes a spouse and brothers and sisters.

**Conflict of interest.** A Financial Interest becomes a conflict of interest only when the board decides that a conflict exists [documents/conflict-of-interest-policy.md, Art. II §2; Art. III §2].

### Procedures for an Interested Person [documents/conflict-of-interest-policy.md, Art. III]

1. **Disclose.** The Interested Person must disclose in writing to the board chair before the meeting, and orally at the meeting (§1).
2. **Leave the meeting** while the remaining directors decide whether a conflict exists (§2).
3. **Do not participate.** The Interested Person may make a presentation, but must then leave for the discussion of, and the vote on, the transaction. The Interested Person does not vote (§3(a)).
4. **Look for alternatives.** The board determines whether a more advantageous arrangement is reasonably available from a party with no conflict (§3(c)).
5. **Make the final findings.** If no such alternative exists, a majority of the disinterested directors present decide whether the transaction is in the Theatre's best interest, for its own benefit, and fair and reasonable (§3(d)).
6. **Compare bids.** For contracts of $10,000 or more, the disinterested directors must compare at least two competing written bids before voting (§4).

### Related requirements

- **Minutes.** The minutes must record the disclosure, the conflict determination, who was present, the discussion (including alternatives), and the vote (Art. V).
- **Annual statements.** Each director must disclose Family Members' interests in bidders on the annual statement and update the statement promptly if circumstances change (Art. VI).

## 5. Schedule L of the 2025 Form 990

### 5.1 Which return and which thresholds apply

**Return year.** The fiscal year from July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026 is reported on the **2025 Form 990**, which is due November 16, 2026. Transactions are tested on the payments made during that fiscal year [documents/schedule-l-part-iv-excerpt.md; documents/controller-email.md].

**Who is an interested person.** For Part IV, interested persons include [documents/schedule-l-part-iv-excerpt.md]:

- directors listed in Part VII;
- their family members, including a spouse and siblings;
- entities more than 35% owned by such persons.

**Reporting thresholds.** A business transaction is reported only if it meets one of these tests [documents/schedule-l-part-iv-excerpt.md]:

- **(a)** total payments exceeding $100,000;
- **(b)** payments from a single transaction exceeding the **greater of $10,000 or 1% of total revenue**;
- **(c)** compensation to a family member exceeding $10,000.

**The single-transaction threshold for the Theatre.** Total revenue on the draft return is $3,940,000 [documents/controller-email.md]. One percent of that is **$39,400**, which is greater than $10,000. The single-transaction threshold is therefore $39,400.

**Reporting does not wait for the Article IV outcome.** Schedule L reporting depends on the relationship and the amounts, not on whether anyone breached the policy. **Possible effect on Part VI.** Article VII also refers to completing Part VI of the Form 990 accurately. The files do not reproduce Part VI's questions. Pryce & Elwood should be told about the undisclosed interest and the Article IV process so they can assess any effect on Part VI.

**Final reporting decisions rest with the preparer.** The paraphrase in the files defers to the IRS instructions [documents/schedule-l-part-iv-excerpt.md]. Pryce & Elwood should make the final reporting determination.

## 6. What the policy requires next

The following steps apply to Saffron & Sage, under Article IV of the policy [documents/conflict-of-interest-policy.md].

1. **Recusal (Art. IV §4).** Mr. Aldana takes no part in the committee's or the board's deliberations or votes on this matter. This applies to his role as a Governance Committee member and as a director.

2. **Reasonable cause and written notice (Art. IV §1).** If the committee finds reasonable cause to believe Mr. Aldana failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict, it **shall** take two steps:
   - inform him in writing of the basis for that belief, citing the records in Section 4.1 and 4.2;
   - give him an opportunity to explain, in writing or at a committee meeting, **within 15 days of the notice**.

3. **Further investigation (Art. IV §2).** After hearing his response, the committee may investigate further. Open questions the files do not answer:
   - whether Celeste M. Aldana is his spouse;
   - whether he knew of the bid when he signed his statement on 2026-01-20;
   - whether he himself has any interest in or compensation from Saffron & Sage;
   - who approved the $1,300 on-site bar upgrade.

4. **Report to the board (Art. IV §2).**
   - The committee reports its findings to the board.
   - The disinterested directors, by majority, determine whether Mr. Aldana failed to disclose.
   - If he did, the board **shall** take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action. The policy lists the options: a written reprimand, removal from committee or officer positions, a request for resignation, or removal under Bylaws Art. IV §7.
   - The choice among these options belongs to the board. This memo does not recommend one.

5. **Re-review of the catering contract (Art. IV §3).** Because the contract was approved without the required disclosure and recusal, it **shall** be re-reviewed by the disinterested directors under Art. III §3(c)–(d).
   - The record before them includes the Harborview bid ($52,900, recommended by the Gala Committee) and the Tidewater bid ($55,750), both for the same specification.
   - If the re-review finds that the Theatre paid more than fair market value, the board **shall** consult legal counsel on two questions: whether the payment may be an excess benefit transaction under IRC §4958, and whether the excess can be recovered.
   - The files do not establish fair market value. That determination is for the re-review.

6. **Corrected annual statement (Art. IV §5; Art. VI).** Mr. Aldana must file a corrected statement within 30 days of the board's determination.

7. **Minutes (Art. V).** The committee and board minutes should record the following:
   - the names of the persons found to have a Financial Interest and the nature of the interest;
   - the determinations made;
   - who was present;
   - the alternatives considered;
   - the votes.

8. **Form 990 (Art. VII).**
   - The committee should authorize the Controller to give Pryce & Elwood the list with both transactions.
   - The Saffron & Sage relationship should be confirmed promptly so the preparer has it in time for the November 16 filing.

### Scheduling note

The policy's deadlines are:

- 15 days for Mr. Aldana's response after notice;
- 30 days after the board's determination for his corrected statement.

The files do not give the date of the next board meeting, so the committee will need to set the reporting timeline itself.
