Here is a comprehensive donor intelligence report on Laurene Powell Jobs, tailored for a mid-sized journalism and media literacy nonprofit based in Washington, D.C. 

---

### **1. Personal and Professional Background**
* **Early Life & Education:** Born November 6, 1963. She earned a B.A. in Political Science and a B.S. in Economics from the University of Pennsylvania (Wharton School) in 1985, followed by an MBA from the Stanford Graduate School of Business in 1991 (Forbes, Wikipedia).
* **Career:** She began her career on Wall Street, working at Merrill Lynch Asset Management and serving as a fixed-income trading strategist at Goldman Sachs. In the 1990s, she co-founded Terravera, a natural foods company (Wikipedia). 
* **Family:** She married Apple co-founder Steve Jobs in 1991. They were married for 20 years until his death in 2011, at which point she inherited the Steven P. Jobs Trust (Wikipedia). 
* **Primary Organization:** She is the founder and president of the Emerson Collective, established in 2011, which blends philanthropy, venture capital, and advocacy (Emerson Collective, Wikipedia). 

### **2. Wealth Indicators and Asset Profile**
* **Net Worth:** Estimated between $11.5 billion (Bloomberg Billionaires Index, Sept 2024) and $13.9 billion (Forbes, 2024).
* **Primary Assets:** Her fortune largely stems from inherited stakes in Apple and The Walt Disney Company. While she has reduced her initial 7.3% stake in Disney over time, these holdings remain the foundation of her wealth (Forbes).
* **Sports Ownership:** In 2017, she purchased a 20% minority stake in Monumental Sports & Entertainment, the parent company of the NBA's Washington Wizards, the NHL's Washington Capitals, the WNBA's Washington Mystics, and the Capital One Arena in Washington, D.C. (Forbes, Wikipedia).
* **Real Estate:** Holds a vast real estate portfolio, including a recently purchased $94 million oceanfront compound in Malibu, California, acquired in June 2024 (Business Insider).

### **3. Known Philanthropic Giving History (Media & Major Gifts)**
Because Powell Jobs primarily uses an LLC structure (Emerson Collective) rather than a traditional 501(c)(3) private foundation, she is not legally required to publicly disclose all grant amounts. However, several major gifts and investments are public knowledge:
* **Media & Journalism Investments:** * Acquired a majority stake in *The Atlantic* in 2017, with an agreement to eventually take full ownership (Wikipedia).
    * Provided undisclosed but significant financial support to nonprofit newsrooms, including *ProPublica*, *The Marshall Project*, and *Mother Jones* (Forbes, InfluenceWatch).
    * Invested in media production and distribution, including podcasting startup Gimlet Media and documentary film company Concordia Studio (Wikipedia).
* **Education ($100M):** Committed $100 million through the Emerson Collective to *XQ: The Super School Project* to redesign high school education in America (Wikipedia).
* **Climate Change ($3.5B):** In 2021, she launched the Waverley Street Foundation with a commitment to distribute $3.5 billion over 10 years to organizations addressing climate change (Forbes).

### **4. Cause Areas and Giving Philosophy**
* **Core Cause Areas:** Media and journalism, education (particularly high school reform and college access for underserved youth), immigration reform, and environmental justice (Emerson Collective).
* **The LLC Model:** Powell Jobs strongly prefers the Limited Liability Company (LLC) model over traditional foundations. This allows her to fluidly mix philanthropic grants, political donations, and for-profit venture capital investments without the strict IRS payout and reporting requirements of a foundation (InfluenceWatch).
* **Giving Philosophy:** She heavily favors anonymity and behind-the-scenes empowerment. In a 2022 interview with Bloomberg, she stated that her philanthropy operates largely anonymously to keep the spotlight on the organizations doing the work, rather than accruing power to the donor (Bloomberg, Wikipedia). Because of this opacity, *Inside Philanthropy* once dubbed her the "Least Transparent Mega-Giver."

### **5. Estimated Giving Capacity**
* **Capacity:** Extremely High. Based on her $11.5B+ net worth and past giving behavior (e.g., her $3.5B climate pledge and $100M education initiative), her capacity for a single transformational gift is effectively limitless. 
* **Context for a D.C. Media Nonprofit:** For a mid-sized organization, she has the clear capacity to comfortably fund a **$1 million to $5 million** multi-year programmatic grant or capacity-building investment, scaling much higher if the initiative aligns with a national strategy.

### **6. Recommended Ask Amount and Engagement Strategy**
* **Recommended Ask Amount:** **$1,000,000 to $2,500,000** structured as a multi-year investment (e.g., $500k/year over 3 to 5 years) to support a specific media literacy initiative or local investigative reporting hub.
* **Engagement Strategy:** * **Do not pitch her directly.** Approach the Emerson Collective's Media and Journalism program directors. 
    * **Frame as an Investment:** The Emerson Collective acts like a venture firm. Your proposal should not read like a standard charity appeal; it should read like a business plan for civic impact. Use terminology like "innovation," "scalability," "civic engagement," and "human flourishing."
    * **Highlight the D.C. Angle:** Given her financial interest in Washington, D.C. via Monumental Sports, framing your nonprofit's work as essential to the civic health of the D.C. metro area can create a direct alignment with her existing regional investments.

### **7. Key Connection Points and Potential Red Flags**
* **Connection Points:**
    * **Washington, D.C. Footprint:** Her ownership in Monumental Sports gives her a vested interest in the D.C. community. Furthermore, *College Track*, the nonprofit she co-founded in 1997, operates a center in the District of Columbia (Business Insider).
    * **Pro-Democracy/Media Literacy Alignment:** She views journalism as a fundamental pillar of democracy, making media literacy a highly complementary field to her existing investments in *The Atlantic* and *ProPublica*.
* **Potential Red Flags:**
    * **Aversion to Traditional Cultivation:** Powell Jobs does not generally care about gala honors, naming rights, or donor plaques. Pitching traditional "donor recognition" benefits will likely fall flat. 
    * **The "Black Box" of the LLC:** Because the Emerson Collective does not publish a Form 990, it is difficult to know exactly when their funding cycles occur or who their current active grantees are. Cultivation may require a long timeline and extensive networking through mutual contacts in the D.C. journalism or tech-philanthropy spheres.
