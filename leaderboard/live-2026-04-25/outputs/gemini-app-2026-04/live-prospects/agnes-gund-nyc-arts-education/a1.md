### **Donor Intelligence Report: Agnes Gund (Estate/Legacy)**

#### **1. Personal and Professional Background**
* **Early Life & Education:** Born August 13, 1938, in Cleveland, Ohio. She earned a BA from Connecticut College in 1960 and an MA in Art History from Harvard University in 1962 (*Source: Wikipedia*).
* **Professional Affiliations:** Gund was a towering figure in the art world. She served as President of the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) from 1991 to 2002 and was later named President Emerita and Life Trustee (*Source: Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper*).
* **Arts Education Legacy:** In 1977, directly in response to severe budget cuts in New York City public schools, she founded **Studio in a School**, a nonprofit that places professional artists as instructors in under-resourced classrooms (*Source: NYC-ARTS, Healing Power of Art*).
* **Family:** She was the heiress to the Gund family fortune (her father was president of Cleveland Trust). She has four children, including filmmaker and philanthropist Catherine Gund (*Source: Wikipedia*).

#### **2. Wealth Indicators and Asset Profile**
* **Family Wealth:** The Gund family holds vast interests in banking, real estate, and public companies (including major stakes in Kellogg's). In 2015, Forbes estimated the broader family's net worth at $3.5 billion (*Source: Wikipedia*).
* **Art Collection & Liquidity:** Gund possessed one of the world's most valuable private art collections. While she promised most of it to museums, Christie’s is scheduled to auction $123 million worth of her remaining art—including masterpieces by Mark Rothko and Cy Twombly—in May 2026 to benefit her designated causes (*Source: Artnet News, Feb 2026*). 
* **Giving Velocity:** During her lifetime, it was reported that she routinely donated roughly two-thirds of her annual income to charitable causes (*Source: Artnet News*).

#### **3. Known Philanthropic Giving History**
* **Art for Justice Fund:** In 2017, Gund sold Roy Lichtenstein’s painting *Masterpiece* to billionaire Steve Cohen for $165 million. She used $100 million of those proceeds to seed the Art for Justice Fund. Over six years, the fund granted over $127 million to more than 200 artists and advocates working to end mass incarceration (*Source: Art for Justice Fund, Ford Foundation*).
* **Museum of Modern Art (MoMA):** She donated over 900 works of art to MoMA and was a primary driver in raising funds for the museum's $858 million expansion in the early 2000s (*Source: Bowdoin College Obituaries, The Art Newspaper*).
* **Studio in a School:** Since its founding, her organization has served over 800,000 children across NYC, currently reaching nearly 30,000 students annually (*Source: Studio in a School, NYC-ARTS*).

#### **4. Cause Areas and Giving Philosophy**
* **Core Causes:** Arts education in public schools, criminal justice reform, contemporary art curation, and reproductive rights.
* **Giving Philosophy:** Gund viewed art and social justice as inextricably linked. She believed that artists and advocates must work together to shift cultural narratives. 
* **Structural Preferences:** She heavily favored "spend-down" or time-limited initiatives. The Art for Justice Fund was intentionally designed to sunset after six years to force rapid, impactful, and agile capital deployment rather than hoarding money in an endowment (*Source: Ford Foundation*).
