## 1. Executive Summary

Laurene Powell Jobs (age 62; born November 6, 1963) stands as one of the most consequential philanthropists and media investors in the United States. As the founder and president of Emerson Collective—an LLC-based social impact organization launched in 2011—and the controlling owner of *The Atlantic* magazine, she has deployed billions toward journalism, education, climate, and democratic infrastructure. Forbes estimates her real-time net worth at **$13.7 billion** as of April 2026, while Bloomberg places it at **$11.9 billion** as of late 2025. Her wealth derives primarily from inherited stakes in Apple and Disney, now diversified through a portfolio that includes premium real estate, media properties, and impact investments.

For a mid-sized journalism and media literacy nonprofit based in Washington, D.C., Powell Jobs represents a **premium major gift prospect** with extraordinary capacity and clear thematic alignment. Her documented giving philosophy treats journalism as a public good essential to democracy, and her ownership of *The Atlantic*—headquartered at the Watergate in Washington, D.C.—demonstrates a concrete, ongoing commitment to the D.C. media ecosystem. However, she operates through opaque vehicles that rarely accept unsolicited proposals. **Estimated annual giving capacity: $50 million–$100 million+. Estimated major gift capacity for a single journalism initiative: $5 million–$25 million.** A critical timing window has opened: her December 2025 divestment of a ~20% stake in Monumental Sports & Entertainment (valued at approximately $1.44 billion) creates immediate liquidity and potential tax-planning motivation.

## 9. Giving Philosophy and Approach

Powell Jobs has articulated a clear, values-driven philosophy that is highly relevant to solicitation strategy:

- **Journalism as public good:** “The importance of news means it should be seen as a ‘public good’… supported by both private and public entities.” (Source: Apple Must, Washington Post)
- **Democracy-risk framing:** “The lack of ability for people to actually find relevant local news is putting our democracy at risk, putting our ability to converse with each other at risk, putting our ability to understand each other at risk.” (Source: MacDailyNews)
- **Independence imperative:** “If an independent media cannot survive independently, it ceases to be an independent media. If independent media becomes a dependent media, who does it depend on?” (Source: Apple Must)
- **Anti-authoritarian philanthropy:** “Giving that expects control is anything but generous.” She has explicitly criticized directive philanthropy and “moral laundering” through charitable giving (Source: EnviroLink, SF Standard)
- **Trust-based practice:** Emerson Collective offers multi-year general operating support with light reporting requirements and anonymous granting when requested (Source: Inside Philanthropy)

**Implication for Solicitation:** Any approach must frame the ask as **partnership for democratic infrastructure**, not as a transactional donation. General operating support requests aligned with systemic journalism resilience will resonate more than restricted program budgets. She responds to urgency around democratic decline and values grantee autonomy.

## 11. Engagement and Solicitation Strategy

### Key Positioning Points
1. **D.C. Ecosystem Anchor:** Her company *The Atlantic* is headquartered in Washington, D.C. She is not a distant Silicon Valley prospect—she has literal corporate infrastructure and editorial leadership in the city.
2. **Democracy-First Framing:** Lead with democratic fragility and local news erosion, not organizational budget needs. She has repeatedly stated that local news collapse threatens democratic discourse.
3. **Media Literacy as Infrastructure:** Position media literacy not as a classroom add-on but as **audience-side infrastructure** that completes the journalistic pipeline she already funds (news production → informed citizenry).
4. **Trust-Based Alignment:** Emphasize general operating support, grantee autonomy, and light reporting. Avoid heavy-handed naming rights or program restrictions.
5. **Youth & Education Nexus:** Connect media literacy to high school and college readiness—bridging her XQ Institute and College Track portfolios with civic education.

### Recommended Engagement Approach

**Phase 1: Relationship Building & Discovery (Months 1–3)**
- Identify a **warm introduction** through *The Atlantic* editorial leadership (D.C.-based), Stanford trustee networks, or Ford Foundation colleagues
- Attend Milken Institute events or Stanford GSB functions where she is a featured speaker (she is scheduled for June 2026 commencement)
- Position initial contact around **democratic infrastructure** and D.C. civic health, not funding requests

**Phase 2: Strategic Cultivation (Months 3–6)**
