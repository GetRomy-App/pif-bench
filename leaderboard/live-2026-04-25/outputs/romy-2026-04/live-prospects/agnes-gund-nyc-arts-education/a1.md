## 1. Executive Summary

**Critical Finding: Agnes Gund passed away on September 18, 2025, at age 87.** Direct individual solicitation is therefore not possible. This report functions as a **posthumous donor intelligence profile** to guide engagement with her surviving philanthropic vehicles, estate, and family heirs.

Agnes Gund (August 13, 1938 – September 18, 2025) was one of the most consequential art patrons and cultural philanthropists in American history. She served as President of the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) from 1991 to 2002, founded the nonprofit **Studio in a School** in 1977 to restore visual arts education to New York City public schools, and established the **AG Foundation** (EIN: 34-1606084), a private foundation that reported **$34.3 million in assets** as of 2024. She famously sold Roy Lichtenstein’s *Masterpiece* (1962) for approximately **$165 million** in 2017, seeding the **Art for Justice Fund** with $100 million to advance criminal justice reform. Over her lifetime, she made an estimated **721 contributions totaling roughly $72.7 million** to 54 cultural institutions between 1999 and 2025.

Her **estimated net worth at death** ranged from **$200 million to $400 million**, anchored by her Manhattan real estate, a remaining art portfolio valued at over **$123 million** (scheduled for Christie’s auction in May 2026), and foundation assets. Her documented **lifetime giving exceeds $72 million**, with additional in-kind donations of more than 800 artworks to institutions including MoMA and the Cleveland Museum of Art.

**For your arts education nonprofit, the actionable opportunity is not with Agnes Gund directly, but with her surviving philanthropic infrastructure:** the AG Foundation, now stewarded by her daughters Catherine Gund and Anna Traggio, and the broader Gund family ecosystem, including the **George Gund Foundation** ($556 million in assets), where Catherine Gund serves as Chair. However, a significant strategic tension exists: Gund already founded **Studio in a School**, the iconic NYC arts education provider that has served over one million public school students. Any approach must demonstrate clear differentiation or partnership value rather than duplication.

## 9. Giving Philosophy and Approach

Agnes Gund practiced **“activist philanthropy”**—she viewed art as a lever for social change, not merely as culture to be preserved. Key tenets of her philosophy include:

- **Asset liquidation for impact:** She sold masterpieces rather than holding them for appreciation, demonstrating a preference for immediate, deployable capital over endowment-building.  
- **Racial justice as personal motivation:** She explicitly tied her criminal justice giving to her family’s racial composition.  
- **Operational founding over check-writing:** She built Studio in a School from scratch rather than merely funding an existing entity.  
- **Bold public positioning:** She made major sales and gifts in response to national political moments (*Dobbs*, mass incarceration crises).  

**Implication for Solicitation:**  
Because Gund is deceased, the relevant “ask” must be directed to her surviving foundation and family. Any approach to the AG Foundation or Catherine Gund must be:  
1. **Mission-bold** — timid, incremental proposals will not resonate.  
2. **Justice-aligned** — framed through equity, access, and systemic change, not just “appreciation” for the arts.  
3. **Differentiated** — must clearly state how you are *not* duplicating Studio in a School, or how you partner with them.
