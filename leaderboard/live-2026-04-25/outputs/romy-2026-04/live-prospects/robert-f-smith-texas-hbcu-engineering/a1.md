## 1. Executive Summary

Robert Frederick Smith (age 63; born December 1, 1962) is the founder, chairman, and CEO of Vista Equity Partners, one of the world’s largest private equity firms specializing in enterprise software. As of March 2026, *Forbes* estimates his net worth at approximately **$28.5 billion**, making him the wealthiest Black American and the richest Black person globally ([Forbes, 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jabariyoung/2026/03/13/the-worlds-black-billionaires//?utm_source=openai)). His **estimated annual giving capacity** comfortably reaches eight figures, with a documented pattern of transformational gifts in the **$15 million to $50 million range** and an endowed initiative approaching **$100 million**.

Smith’s philanthropic identity is tightly bound to HBCUs, STEM education for underrepresented minorities, and student debt liberation. In 2019, he stunned the nation by paying off the approximately **$34 million** in student loan debt held by the entire Morehouse College graduating class and their families ([Morehouse College News](https://news.morehouse.edu/inside-morehouse/student-success-program-receives-34-million-donation-from-philanthropist-robert-f-smith-for-class-of-2019?utm_source=openai)). He subsequently launched the **Student Freedom Initiative (SFI)** with a **$100 million endowment**—half personally and half via his **Fund II Foundation**—to provide income-contingent funding, mentoring, and internships for STEM students at HBCUs and Minority-Serving Institutions ([Student Freedom Initiative](https://studentfreedominitiative.org/donors/?utm_source=openai)). He is the first Black American signatory of the Giving Pledge.

For a Texas HBCU engineering program, Smith represents a **premium major gift prospect**. He resides in **Austin, Texas**, and his SFI already counts **Prairie View A&M University** as a partner institution—a powerful existing connection point. His giving philosophy favors scalable, data-driven interventions that dismantle systemic barriers. A solicitation framed around student debt relief, paid internships through his **internXL** platform, and digital infrastructure equity aligns precisely with his demonstrated priorities. Recommended ask range: **$10 million–$25 million** for a named endowed engineering fund, with stretch potential to **$50 million** for a transformational center or school naming.

## 9. Giving Philosophy and Approach

Robert F. Smith’s giving is best understood as **philanthropic private equity**: he deploys capital into ecosystems, not just organizations. He favors:
- **Scale:** Gifts that can touch thousands of students (e.g., SFI’s 34-campus expansion).
- **Leverage:** Matching structures (SFI’s $50M/$50M match; Cisco partnerships) that multiply his capital.
- **Measurable outcomes:** His NAF gift was explicitly tied to internship placements and student performance metrics.
- **Systemic intervention:** Rather than funding single scholarships, he targets the **student debt instrument itself**, digital infrastructure gaps, and internship access barriers.
- **Racial equity as north star:** Nearly every major initiative centers Black, Brown, and underrepresented students.

**Implication for Solicitation:**
- A direct ask for “annual support” or a generic scholarship will fail. The pitch must present a **scalable, data-driven intervention** that removes a specific barrier for HBCU engineering students.
- He responds to **partnership language** (“co-invest,” “match,” “leverage”) rather than donor-beneficiary charity framing.
- Naming matters: The Cornell engineering school naming indicates he values institutional legacy and permanent recognition.
